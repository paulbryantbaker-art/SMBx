# Value Creation Plan

## When to use
In the weeks before close (the plan is presented to the seller-transition and key employees in week one) and quarterly thereafter. SMB post-close reality differs from PE playbooks in one decisive way: the first 100 days are dominated by *transfer* — the seller's transition, the customers' confidence, the crews' decision to stay — and value creation begun before stabilisation is complete destroys value.

## House rules
- **Engine:** bridge arithmetic and DSCR tracking are engine runs against the deal profile as actuals replace assumptions. The plan inherits the IC memo's verified base — it does not restate its own numbers.
- **Evidence:** each bridge lever carries a confidence rating grounded in diligence findings, not ambition.

## Method
1. **100-day plan, four phases.**
   - *Stabilise (days 1–30):* seller transition agreement activated and visible (the seller introducing the buyer, not vanishing); retention bonuses landed with the key people from the dependency map; top-customer contact plan executed (in person for anyone above 10%); license bridge and permits confirmed operative; payroll, banking, insurance, bonding switched without a gap; suppliers notified with terms preserved.
   - *Understand (days 15–60):* ride along on jobs; sit with the books weekly; map how work is actually priced, scheduled, and collected; find what diligence couldn't see from documents — the real estimator, the real bottleneck, the customers already wobbling.
   - *Build (days 30–90):* financial reporting to a monthly close the engine can consume; the first-layer hires the dependency map called Absent; job costing if none exists; the review cadence with the client/operator.
   - *Begin (days 60–100):* two or three high-confidence, low-disruption levers only — a supported price correction, collections discipline, supplier terms. Nothing that spooks crews or customers in the first quarter.
   Every action: owner, deadline, success criterion, dependency.
2. **Earnings bridge by lever.** Decompose the path from acquired verified earnings to the exit/target figure: *organic revenue growth* (at actual gross margin), *gross margin improvement* (pricing, mix, job costing discipline, purchasing), *operating leverage* (fixed cost growing slower than revenue), *cost reduction* (named line items, not "efficiencies"), *add-on M&A* (if in thesis: assumed multiple, integration owner, synergy stated separately and skeptically). Each lever: dollar contribution, share of bridge, confidence (high/medium/low) tied to a diligence finding, and its primary delivery risk. The bridge must sum; the engine checks it.
3. **Workstreams.** One accountable owner each — in an SMB that owner is often the operator wearing two hats, so cap concurrent workstreams at what the bench can carry (usually three). Milestones are output-based with dates; KPIs are leading where possible.
4. **KPI dashboard, three horizons.** *Monthly pulse:* booked jobs / new contracts, backlog in weeks, gross margin per job, technician/crew utilization, callback or warranty rate, AR days, cash. *Trailing 12-month trend:* each pulse metric as a line — the inflection matters more than the month. *Leading indicators:* quote volume and win rate, referral source activity, crew headcount vs plan, seller-transition hours remaining. Plus *actuals vs plan* (revenue, earnings, FCF — never actuals alone) and *DSCR tracking* against the 1.25/1.50 thresholds every month there is acquisition debt.
5. **Quarterly review.** Lever-by-lever: planned vs actual contribution, trajectory vs required, refresh the bridge with engine-run actuals, name what is behind and the corrective action, and capture new levers found in operation. Feed durable lessons back to the studio's method docs (pattern only — nothing counterparty-confidential crosses the seam).

## Inputs
IC memo (thesis, verified base, risk register); dependency map and retention plan; seller transition agreement; management's or operator's plan; monthly actuals as they land.

## Output
The four-phase 100-day plan with owners and criteria; the lever bridge with confidence and risks; workstream definitions; the dashboard specification; the quarterly review template.
