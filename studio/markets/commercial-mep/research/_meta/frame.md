# Commercial MEP — hunt frame

**Opened 2026-08-03.** Hunt B: how this market works. Not a client list (A) and
not a target list (C).

## Why this is its own market and not a section of home services

`markets/home-services/master.md` §4.3 tables commercial-mechanical platforms
separately, and §4.1's framework caveat says why: they "trade on backlog and
data-center logic", not on a residential maintenance book. §2.2 makes the same
point from the other side — electrical's 21.6% large-enterprise share is
concentrated in commercial and data-centre revenue, which is precisely why
residential electrical reads as *less* consolidated than the aggregate implies.

Two markets, two underwriting models, two masters.

## The first questions to test — all currently UNVERIFIED

Each of these comes from the CDaaS strategy document filed beside this frame.
None is on disk as evidence. Treat every one as a hypothesis.

1. **Is there really no national pure-play commercial plumbing consolidator?**
   The strongest claim in the source document and the whole basis of its
   whitespace thesis. Test it against the register, not against commentary.
2. **Do data-centre certifications actually command the quoted premium?**
   The document cites Dycom at 9.7x and CBRE deploying $1.2B. Neither figure is
   on disk. Find the filings.
3. **Does the "service conversion" arbitrage hold on commercial work?** The
   home-services master's recurring-revenue thesis is residential; §8.4 already
   shows the recurring threshold is quoted at four different values by one
   publisher. Whether any of that transfers to commercial mechanical is unknown.
4. **What is the commercial share of NAICS 238220 nationally**, not just in
   Texas? The 24.3% kind-of-business split is a Texas figure.
