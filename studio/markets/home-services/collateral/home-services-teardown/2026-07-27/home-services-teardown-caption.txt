Everyone sees the home-services roll-up. Fewer read the buyer math underneath it.

I spent this week verifying every number in my own home-services research against primary sources. Six figures did not survive — including a "$1.2 trillion of dry powder hunting essential services" line that turns out to be an all-sector total someone relabeled, and a market-size figure attributed to the Census that is not in the Census.

So this teardown leads with what actually holds up: about 89% of plumbing and HVAC shops have fewer than twenty employees, three quarters of critical-trade operators are still independent, and in plumbing nobody owns 2% of the country. The fragmentation was always the thesis. The aggregate market size was never doing the work.

The rest of it — the 4–8x to 16–20x re-rate, the 40% recurring-revenue threshold, the 2.1 million unfilled trades jobs by 2030, and the trap where the technicians walk at closing — is in the carousel, six trades at a time.

One page has no number on it at all. Electrical. Every published figure I could find measures the wrong thing, so I say that instead of picking one.

If you are buying in one of these trades, or operating in one, I would like to hear what you are seeing on the ground. I read everything.
