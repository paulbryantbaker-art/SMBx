# Run 04 (Hunt B) — Who Is Consolidating the US Elevator Service Base

**Scope.** PE-backed and strategic acquirers of independent US elevator service companies. Primary deliverable: adjudicate the practice's already-posted claim that "PE services ~10% of US elevator units."

---

## PART 0 — THE ~10% CLAIM: VERDICT

### Verdict: **CANNOT BE TRACED — retire as a cited fact; may be re-issued as a labelled Estimate.**

**What I looked for and did not find.** No published source I could reach states that private equity services approximately 10% of US elevator units. I ran eight distinct search formulations against the figure (see header) and fetched every candidate that surfaced. The specific sources that *should* carry such a number, and what they actually say:

|---|---|---|

Calls the market "highly fragmented" but explicitly does not quantify OEM vs independent share. | Disclosed |
| STEER Partners elevator newsletter, June 2024 — https://steerpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/STEER-Partners-Elevator-Newsletter-Final-Draft.pdf | **Nothing.** Carries a six-row PE platform table (sponsors, HQ, headcount, add-on counts) but no aggregate unit-share percentage. | Disclosed |
| CT Acquisitions, "Elevator Company M&A Multiples Report 2026" — https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/elevator-ma-multiples-2026/ | **Nothing.** Explicitly no aggregate market-share figure for PE platforms. | Disclosed |
| Houlihan Lokey, Facility & Residential Services Market Update Q3 2025 — http://cdn.hl.com/pdf/2025/facility-and-residential-services-update-q3-2025.pdf | **Nothing elevator-specific.** Lists "Elevators and Escalators" as a coverage subsector and names two elevator deals; no unit-share data, no elevator deal count, no elevator multiples. | Disclosed |

**The one unresolved candidate.** The single plausible origin is an Elevator World feature titled **"Private Equity Consolidation of the North American Elevator Service Industry Since 2018"** — https://elevatorworld.com/article/private-equity-consolidation-of-the-north-american-elevator-service-industry-since-2018/. It ranked first for **every one of the six share-related queries I ran**, which is exactly the fingerprint of a claim's source. **I could not read it.** Elevator World returns HTTP 403 on its `/article/` path to direct fetch, to a reader proxy, and the Wayback Machine returned 403/429. Note the diagnostic: Elevator World's `/news/daily-news/` path fetches fine (I pulled six such items successfully) — only the paid magazine `/article/` path is walled. So the article exists and is paywalled magazine content, not vapour.

**I did not read it, so I do not know whether it contains "~10%," and I will not assume it does.** Its base year and denominator are therefore both unknown. If the practice's claim was sourced from this article, the citation is currently unverifiable by the practice as well, since the article is paywalled — which is itself a reason not to keep asserting the number publicly.

### Independent bottom-up reconstruction (Estimated — my derivation, not a published figure)

Because the claim could not be traced, I rebuilt it from the ground up. Denominator: **1.03M US elevators** (NEII, 2020 fact sheet — note this is a 2020-vintage denominator and the only industry-body figure I found; Stax's "over 1M" for 2024 is consistent).

| Platform (sponsor) | Units under service | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| American Elevator Group (Arcline) | 30,000 | **Disclosed** — company's own site, "30,000 elevators," 12 partner companies, 22 states |
| Specialized Elevator (Berkshire Partners) | ~25,000 | **Disclosed** — company release, 7 May 2026, "approximately 25,000 elevator and escalator units," 30 markets, 500+ IUEC |
| Axxiom Elevator (Gauge Capital) | 8,000–12,000 | **Estimated** — 13 acquisitions across 8 states, no unit count published |
| Action Elevator (H.I.G. Capital) | 4,000–7,000 | **Estimated** — "approximately 500 customers"/"roughly 800 facilities" disclosed; units not published |
| Champion Elevator (Thayer Street) | 4,000–7,000 | **Estimated** — Westech add-on alone was 775 units; 5 add-ons total |
| Elevator Service, Inc. (Carroll Capital) | 4,000–7,000 | **Estimated** — 14 states, no unit count published |
| Total Access Elevator (Century Park) | 2,000–4,000 | **Estimated** — SoCal only, 3 add-ons |
| Integrity Elevator Solutions (Del Monte) | 1,000–2,000 | **Estimated** — TX/LA only |
| Urban Elevator (Skydeck, residual) | 1,000–2,000 | **Estimated** — 3 remaining IL/IN locations after Otis bought 8 |
| Ascend Safety Collective (Altaline / Haven) | — | Launched 12 May 2026; no scale disclosed |
| **Total** | **~79,000–96,000** | **Estimated** |

**~79k–96k / 1.03M = roughly 8–9% of US elevator units.**

### So what survives

1. **As a citation: nothing.** There is no traceable published source. Stop attributing it.
2. **As a magnitude: it is defensible, and it was probably *more* defensible two years ago than today.** Add Elevated Facility Services Group (~$220M revenue, 22 states, 30+ markets — Estimated 30,000–40,000 units) and the total crosses ~11–12%. Elevated **was** PE-owned (L Squared) until **4 June 2024**. So a figure of "~10%" is most consistent with a **base year of 2023 or H1 2024**, before Elevated left private equity.
3. **It is now going stale in the wrong direction.** Two of the largest PE-backed platforms have exited to strategic acquirers inside 14 months — Elevated to APi Group (June 2024) and eight of Urban Elevator's locations to Otis (April 2025). Units are moving *out* of PE and *into* strategics. A "PE share" number published today and dated today overstates PE.
4. **The claim is also denominator-ambiguous in a way that can be attacked.** TK Elevator has been owned by a private equity consortium (Advent and Cinven, via Vertical Topco I S.A.) since 2020 and is a top-four US player; EQT tendered for Fujitec in July 2025. If "private equity" is read to include PE-owned OEMs, the true figure is far above 10%. If it means "PE-backed roll-ups of independents," it is ~8–9%. The posted claim does not say which. **That ambiguity is the most likely way this gets challenged publicly.**

**Recommended replacement language:** "PE-backed roll-ups of independent elevator service companies control an estimated 8–9% of the ~1.03M US elevator units (smbX estimate, built bottom-up from disclosed platform unit counts; excludes PE-owned OEMs such as TK Elevator)."

## PART C — WHERE THE CONSOLIDATORS ARE NOT

This is the commercial part of the pass.

### C1. Size floors — the hard gate

The clearest published floors, from CT Acquisitions (advisory source; directionally consistent with the platform behaviour observed above):
- **"750+ units under contract is the entry point for a real sponsor process; 2,000+ is platform-eligible"**
- **"$1M to $3M [EBITDA] is the entry band where sponsor add-on platforms run a competitive process"**; $3–10M is the "dense-route multi-state tier"; $10M+ is "platform-candidate range"

Corroboration from observed deals: Champion's Westech add-on was **775+ units** — right at that stated floor. **Below ~750 contracted units, an owner has no sponsor process at all.** That is a very large population of companies with no institutional exit path, and it is the practice's natural client base.

### C2. Geography — where sponsor demand concentrates, and where it doesn't

Sponsor demand concentrates in **NY tri-state, Southern California, Bay Area, Boston, DC, Chicago, Texas (Houston/Dallas/Austin), Philadelphia, Miami**; "stranded geographies discount." The platform footprints confirm this precisely:

- **Total Access:** three add-ons in eight months, **all within Greater Los Angeles** (Simi Valley, LA, Van Nuys)
- **Action Elevator (H.I.G.):** every add-on in the **DC/MD/VA corridor**
- **Champion:** exclusively **NY metro / CT / NJ**
- **Specialized:** 30 markets but anchored on Boston, NY, CA, Philadelphia
- **Axxiom:** FL, AZ, CA, TX, DC, and now the Carolinas/GA — the broadest, and the only platform actively opening *new* regions

**Whitespace, stated as the states with no verified platform presence.** Aggregating every footprint above (AEG 22 states; Specialized 30 markets; Axxiom AZ/CA/FL/TX/DC/SC/NC/GA; ESI 14 states concentrated MI/WI/IL/IN/OH/MN/MO/LA; Action MD/DC/VA; Champion NY/CT/NJ; Total Access SoCal; Integrity TX/LA), the consistently uncovered territory is:

- **Interior Mountain West** — Montana, Wyoming, Idaho, Utah, Nevada outside Las Vegas, New Mexico outside the IronHawk footprint
- **Northern Plains and Upper Midwest outside metros** — the Dakotas, Nebraska, Iowa, Kansas
- **Interior South and Appalachia** — Arkansas, Oklahoma, Kentucky outside Madden's footprint, West Virginia beyond Specialized's WV branch, Tennessee, Alabama, Mississippi
- **Northern New England** — Vermont, New Hampshire, Maine (ATIS bought a Vermont *inspection* business — that is inspections, not maintenance, and it underlines that no maintenance platform is there)
- **Alaska and Hawaii** — no platform presence found

### C3. Segments the platforms avoid

- **Install-heavy / modernization-heavy books.** Explicitly a deal-breaker: "install-heavy revenue with a thin maintenance portfolio." Install/mod-heavy shops trade **2.5x–6.0x** vs maintenance-heavy. Sponsors are buying **recurring maintenance contracts**, not construction backlog. APi bought Elevated substantially because **~70% of revenue was inspection/service/repair**.
- **Residential and accessibility lifts.** Left almost entirely to non-PE strategics — **Cibes Lift Group** (4 US buys), **Harmar Mobility**, **Savaria**. The commercial platforms are not competing here.
- **Components and parts manufacturing.** A separate, differently-sponsored lane: Standard Elevator Systems (Arcline), Elevator Systems Inc. (ShoreView), MEI. Do not screen these against service buy-boxes.
- **Low route density regardless of size.** "Route density" is the recurring valuation term. A $3M-EBITDA company spread thinly over four states is worth materially less to these buyers than a $2M company concentrated in one metro.

### C4. Other stated deal-breakers (diligence-stage, not screening-stage)

High callback rates (read as "deferred maintenance, thin route staffing, or quality problems"); **orphaned proprietary controllers facing obsolescence**; loss of licensed mechanics; customer concentration; poor safety records or open code violations; owner-dependency; messy accounting.

### C5. The structural opening

Three findings compound into the practice's actual pitch:

1. **PE is a net seller of scale in this sector right now.** Elevated → APi (Jun 2024). Urban's eight locations → Otis (Apr 2025). The two largest transfers of units in the last two years both moved *from* PE *to* strategics.
2. **The OEMs are about to be distracted.** KONE/TKE will be in antitrust review until at least Q2 2027. Otis and Schindler continue tuck-ins, but the two largest players are occupied.
3. **The below-750-unit population has no buyer.** Sponsors will not run a process; OEMs buy portfolios not companies. That population is geographically concentrated in exactly the whitespace in C2.

There is also a **labour-model split** worth screening on: Specialized is explicitly IUEC-union ("employer of choice for union mechanics," 500+ IUEC members) and Urban called itself "the largest union independent." Others make no such claim. **Union vs open-shop is a hard compatibility gate on any add-on match** and should be a field in the target screen.

## What we don't know yet

1. **Whether the ~10% claim has a real source.** The one candidate — Elevator World's "Private Equity Consolidation of the North American Elevator Service Industry Since 2018" — is paywalled and returned 403 to direct fetch, to a reader proxy, and via the Wayback Machine. **Its base year, denominator and actual figure are unknown to me.** Resolving this requires a paid Elevator World subscription. Until then the claim is untraceable and should not be re-published as a citation.
2. **Unit counts for seven of the ten PE platforms.** Only AEG (30,000) and Specialized (~25,000) publish them. Axxiom, Action, ESI, Total Access, Integrity, Ascend and Champion publish none. The 8–9% reconstruction is therefore an estimate resting on two disclosed numbers and seven of mine.
3. **Gauge Capital's investment date in Axxiom.** Not published on Gauge's portfolio page; the Mergr transaction page returned empty. Axxiom's founding year is likewise unpublished.
4. **Domains for seven platforms** — Action Elevator, Elevator Service Inc. (Carroll), Total Access Elevator, Integrity Elevator Solutions, Ascend Safety Collective, ATIS, Ascent Elevators' sponsor. **Left empty in the register rather than guessed.**
5. **The Align/Maven conflict on Century Elevator.** Align claims two add-ons during its hold (Avery, Century); Maven's ledger places Century under H.I.G. in 2024. Unresolved.
6. **Champion's Superior Elevator Technologies acquisition date.** Elevator World carries the item without a visible date. Left undated.
7. **Whether TK Elevator's US maintenance base should count as "PE-owned"** for the practice's public framing. This is an editorial decision, not a research gap — but it must be made explicitly, because it swings the headline number from ~8% to well over 20%.
8. **No industry-wide elevator M&A deal count exists.** Maven's 51-deal ledger is the best available and is a biased sample of one advisor's mandates.
9. **Escalators are essentially unaddressed** in the consolidator literature. NEII gives 56,000 escalators in North America; no platform publishes an escalator-specific strategy. Possible unexamined niche.
10. **AEG's 22-state list is not enumerated** on its own site (only NY, NJ, PA are named), so the whitespace map in C2 is built from the 19 unnamed states being inferred from add-on locations. A subscription database would tighten this.
