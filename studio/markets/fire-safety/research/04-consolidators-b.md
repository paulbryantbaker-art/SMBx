# HEADLINE STRUCTURAL FINDING (read this first)

**Fire alarm is not a fire sub-vertical at the classification level, at the company level, or at the bidding level. It is a security sub-vertical that fire buyers are permitted to visit.**

NAICS 561621 — *Security Systems Services (except Locksmiths)* — is defined verbatim as:

> "This U.S. industry comprises establishments primarily engaged in (1) selling security alarm systems, such as burglar and fire alarms, along with installation, repair, or monitoring services or (2) remote monitoring of electronic security alarm systems."
> — NAICS 561621 official definition, `Basis: Disclosed`, https://www.naics.com/naics-code-description/?code=561621 (code text unchanged across 2007/2012/2017/2022 NAICS revisions)

Index entries under the same code include both **"Fire alarm monitoring services"** and **"Burglar alarm sales combined with installation, repair, or monitoring services."** There is no separate NAICS code for commercial fire alarm contracting. A fire-focused buyer screening on NAICS will pull burglar-alarm companies; a security-focused buyer screening on the same code will pull fire alarm companies. **They are screening the same list.**

The consequence, established in Layer 3 below with named parties: in fire alarm and fire monitoring, a fire-platform buyer is bidding against electronic-security platforms capitalised by Ares, Leonard Green, Harvest, GTCR, Wind Point, Morgan Stanley Capital Partners, Access Holdings and Tenex — **and against Securitas AB, which paid $3,200 million for a single electronic security business.** In extinguisher/kitchen (Layer 2) that competition largely disappears, and the fire buyer's competition is other fire route-density buyers plus Cintas.

## 1.14 RMR MULTIPLES vs EBITDA MULTIPLES — THE TWO LANGUAGES

**These are measures of different things and are not interchangeable. Stating this plainly is part of the brief.**

- An **EBITDA multiple** prices the *whole enterprise*: install revenue, service revenue, monitoring revenue, and the overhead that supports all three.
- An **RMR multiple** prices *only the contracted monthly recurring monitoring stream*, as a multiple of one month's revenue. It is a **book-of-accounts price**, historically used because alarm accounts were bought and sold like receivables, independent of the business that installed them. Install/service revenue is typically **excluded** and valued separately (or at zero).

### Hard, sourced RMR multiples

**Michael Barnes, Founding Partner, Barnes Associates — presented at ESX 2025 (article dated 2025-08-18):**

| Seller size | 2024 transaction RMR multiple | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Companies **under $50,000 RMR** | **36x** monthly revenue | Press-derived |
| Companies **above $500,000 RMR** | **46x** monthly revenue | Press-derived |

Also from the same presentation (`Basis: Press-derived`):
- US security alarm integrator market: **$78 billion** — **$41 billion** sales/installation + **$37 billion** recurring revenue (vs **$37 billion** total 20 years earlier).
- **"approximately 2% of industry RMR changed hands"** in the acquisition year discussed.
- Growth by segment: traditional systems ≈**3%** · video **"16%-plus growth that continues accelerating"** · large commercial projects **11%** · smart home **13%**.
- Gross margins on recurring revenue declined from **54% (2015)**; recent stabilisation via **3–4% annual price increases**; inflation since 2020 ≈**27%**.
- Market share **by RMR**: national **48%** · regional **12%** · local **23%** · smaller players **25%**.

**Barnes Buchanan Security Conference, 31st annual, 2026-02-16, The Breakers, West Palm Beach** (article dated 2026-02-16, `Basis: Press-derived`):
- Security alarm industry CAGR 2020–2025: **6%**.
- Specialty segment growth: large commercial **12%** · video **16%** · smart home **13%** · **traditional intrusion/fire 3%**.
- **"the average dollar in the industry statistically produced 12 cents of billable service revenue."**
- Average monitoring/services margin **51.2%** (down from **53.9%** in 2017).
- Market share at end-2025: national **48%** · regional **11%** · local **23%**; local+regional fell from **50% (2016)** to **34% (2025)**.
- **"about 21% of the industry has changed hands in the last four years."**
- Presenters: **Mike Barnes** (Barnes Associates), **Alper Cetingok** (Raymond James), **Hart Brown** (Saxum); panellists **Steve Butkovich** (CPI), **Daniel Oppenheim** (Affiliated Monitoring), **Brent Uhl** (Guardian Alarm).

### The scale premium, quantified

A seller above $500k RMR clears at **46x** versus **36x** for a seller under $50k RMR — **10 turns of RMR, a 27.8% uplift** (D-8). This is the *arithmetic case for the roll-up* in monitoring: buy sub-scale books at 36x, aggregate, and the same RMR is worth 46x. It is also a warning — a fire buyer purchasing small alarm books is buying at the *cheap* end only if it can actually consolidate them onto one central station.

### Translating between the two languages (and why you should be careful)

`Basis: Estimated` — arithmetic in D-9. Using the **sourced** 51.2% monitoring/services margin from Barnes Buchanan 2026:
- **46x RMR = 3.83x annualised monitoring revenue = ≈7.5x annualised monitoring gross profit.**
- **36x RMR = 3.00x annualised monitoring revenue = ≈5.9x annualised monitoring gross profit.**

**These are NOT EBITDA multiples.** They are multiples of *gross* profit on the monitoring line only, before any selling, branch, G&A or corporate cost. The true EV/EBITDA equivalent is necessarily **higher** than 7.5x, by an amount that depends entirely on the acquirer's ability to absorb the accounts with zero incremental overhead — which is exactly why a strategic with an owned central station pays 46x and a financial buyer without one cannot.

**Underwriting instruction:** do not convert an RMR multiple to an EBITDA multiple and present the result as a valuation. Price the RMR book at an RMR multiple, price the install/service business at an EBITDA multiple, and add them. Anyone who quotes you a single blended multiple for an alarm company with meaningful install revenue is hiding something.

## 2.13 The kitchen/hood ownership boundary — mapped

**This is the messiest ownership boundary in the industry, and mapping it was an explicit part of the brief. Here is what the evidence supports.**

Commercial kitchen fire safety splits into two services that are **regulated together (NFPA 96) but owned apart**:
1. **Wet-chemical suppression system service** (semi-annual inspection of the Ansul/Pyro-Chem-type hood system) — a **fire licence** activity.
2. **Kitchen exhaust / hood & duct cleaning** (grease removal) — **not** a fire licence activity in most jurisdictions.

**Three distinct ownership models observed, with named examples:**

| Model | Named example | Owner | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Both inside a fire platform** | **Guardian Fire Protection Services** — lists kitchen suppression, vent hood fire systems, portable extinguishers **and exhaust hood cleaning** | **Knox Lane** | Disclosed (company site) |
| **(a) Both inside a fire platform** | **Pye-Barker Fire & Safety** — operates a dedicated **Kitchen Hood Cleaning** service line alongside fire suppression | **Altas Partners / LGP / ADIA / GIC** | Disclosed (company site) |
| **(a) Both inside a fire platform** | **National Fire & Safety** — services include **kitchen hood systems** plus extinguisher maintenance and central-station monitoring | **Highview Capital** | Disclosed |
| **(b) Hood cleaning inside a *franchised commercial cleaning* platform, no fire licence** | **Green Guard** (KEC) under **EverSmith Brands** | **The Riverside Company** | Disclosed |
| **(b) Hood cleaning inside a franchise system** | **HOODZ** (Hoodz International, LLC) under **BELFOR Franchise Group** | BELFOR Franchise Group (ultimate sponsor **not verified** — see §"What I could not verify") | Press-derived |
| **(c) Independent hood-cleaning specialists, no fire platform** | **Facilitec USA** / **Facilitec West** / **Facilitec SW**; **Enviromatic Corporation**; **Kitchen Guard** (franchised, first franchise agreement announced 2024) | **Ownership not verified for any of these** | — |

**The finding a buyer needs:** the *suppression* half of the kitchen route is consistently held by fire platforms; the *cleaning* half is fragmented across fire platforms **and** franchised commercial-cleaning platforms **and** independents. **The franchised cleaning platforms (Riverside's EverSmith/Green Guard, BELFOR's HOODZ) are not fire companies and do not compete for fire assets — but they are buying the same customer relationship at the same restaurant.** A fire buyer acquiring kitchen route density will find the cleaning half of the route already sold, to a buyer it never saw in a process, priced on a **franchising** model rather than an ITM model.

Riverside is simultaneously running a **fire extinguisher platform (Relay)**, a **franchised kitchen-exhaust-cleaning platform (EverSmith/Green Guard)**, and until Q1 2026 a **fire ITM platform (CertaSite)**. Riverside has mapped this boundary already.

## 3.2 Are commercial security integrators paying security multiples or fire multiples for fire alarm work?

**Evidence, and it is uncomfortable for the "fire trades at a premium" thesis:**

- The **only disclosed multiple on a large commercial fire-and-security asset** is **Everon/ADT Commercial at 11.2x** Commercial Adjusted EBITDA (2023-08-08, ADT primary). That asset was **≈19% of ADT's revenue** and explicitly **"commercial security, fire and life safety."**
- The **only disclosed fire-platform multiple range** is **17x–20x on $350M EBITDA for Pye-Barker** — and it is **PE Hub via SDM, unconfirmed, company declined comment, and did not result in a sale.**
- **APi Group's disclosed bolt-on discipline:** *"weighted average adjusted EBITDA multiple for bolt-on acquisitions <6x in each year"* from **2019–2024** (APi Investor Day, 2025-05-21, p.45). `Basis: Disclosed.`

**Reading these three together — which is the whole point of this section:**

| Transaction type | Multiple | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Tuck-in** fire/life-safety acquisition by a public strategic | **<6x** adj. EBITDA (weighted avg, 6 consecutive years) | Disclosed |
| **Large commercial fire + security platform**, disclosed | **11.2x** adj. EBITDA | Disclosed |
| **Fire ITM platform**, unconfirmed sellside aspiration | **17–20x** EBITDA | Press-derived, denied comment, no sale |

**Conclusion I would underwrite on:** security integrators are **not** paying a fire premium for fire alarm work — the disclosed 11.2x for a fire-and-security business sits *below* the aspirational fire-platform range and well above the strategic tuck-in range. **The premium is a function of scale and recurring mix, not of the word "fire."** A sub-$5M-EBITDA fire alarm business is a <6x-to-high-single-digit asset regardless of who buys it; the multiple expansion happens on the way *up* the size curve, which is the arbitrage the sponsors listed in §3.1 are all harvesting.

- The gross RMR is **not** the margin. The wholesale fee is a direct cost, and the Barnes Buchanan **51.2% monitoring/services margin** is a blended industry figure that a pure reseller will not achieve.
- The **contract with the subscriber** may or may not be freely assignable, and the wholesale agreement will have its own change-of-control provisions.
- You are paying an **RMR multiple of 36x** (Barnes, sub-$50k RMR sellers) for a stream whose infrastructure is owned by a Graham Partners portfolio company.

**This is the single most actionable diligence item in this file.** "Does the target own its central station, and if not, whose paper is the RMR on?" changes the price by roughly ten turns of RMR.

**Where a fire-focused buyer is *not* outbid — and should concentrate:**
- **Special hazard / clean agent** (ORR-type, **8.9% annualised RMR/revenue**): low RMR means the security integrators' central-station synergy is worth nothing here, and they mostly do not have the engineering licences. Competition is other fire buyers plus Cintas.
- **Extinguisher-only routes with no monitoring**: no RMR, therefore no RMR-multiple bidder.
- **Sub-$3M-EBITDA fire alarm service businesses in secondary metros**: below the screening threshold of the Ares/GTCR/Wind Point-class platforms, and the disclosed strategic tuck-in benchmark is **<6x**.

# Derivations

All arithmetic below is mine. Inputs are cited above and are Disclosed or Press-derived as labelled; outputs are `Basis: Estimated`.

**D-1 — Everon / ADT Commercial: implied corporate cost allocation.**
ADT disclosed EV **$1,600,000,000** and **"approximately 11.2X"** EV/Commercial Adjusted EBITDA *including estimated corporate cost allocation*, on LTM Commercial Adjusted EBITDA of **$158,000,000** (to 2023-06-30).
- EV ÷ unallocated EBITDA = $1,600,000,000 ÷ $158,000,000 = **10.13x**
- Implied *allocated* EBITDA = $1,600,000,000 ÷ 11.2 = **$142,857,143**
- Implied corporate cost allocation = $158,000,000 − $142,857,143 = **$15,142,857**
→ The headline 11.2x is struck on EBITDA **after** ≈$15.1m of allocated corporate cost. On the segment's own reported EBITDA the deal is **10.1x**. *Use 10.1x when comparing against a standalone target that carries its own overhead.*

**D-2 — Annualised RMR as a share of revenue.** Formula: (RMR × 12) ÷ FY2025 revenue. Inputs: SDM 100 2026 (company-reported, as of 2025-12-31).
- ADT: ($358,700,000 × 12) = $4,304,400,000 ÷ $4,354,000,000 = **98.86%**
- Vector Security: ($20,075,000 × 12) = $240,900,000 ÷ $454,590,000 = **52.99%**
- Zeus: ($6,467,326 × 12) = $77,607,912 ÷ $205,859,618 = **37.70%**
- Securitas Technology: ($102,000,000 × 12) = $1,224,000,000 ÷ $3,459,840,000 = **35.38%** *(both inputs SDM-estimated "e")*
- Everon: ($43,340,856 × 12) = $520,090,272 ÷ $1,618,725,121 = **32.13%**
- Pye-Barker: ($50,391,844 × 12) = $604,702,128 ÷ $2,000,000,000 = **30.24%**
- Pavion: ($17,832,431 × 12) = $213,989,172 ÷ $904,972,750 = **23.65%**
- Minuteman: ($1,986,667 × 12) = $23,840,004 ÷ $202,454,000 = **11.78%**
- ORR Protection: ($1,254,129 × 12) = $15,049,548 ÷ $169,428,932 = **8.88%**
- Security 101: ($1,522,313 × 12) = $18,267,756 ÷ $300,765,253 = **6.07%**
- RapidFire: ($1,188,586 × 12) = $14,263,032 ÷ $71,925,249 = **19.83%**
- Kastle: ($12,800,000 × 12) = $153,600,000 ÷ $282,000,000 = **54.47%**

**D-3 — ADT Commercial EV / revenue.**
$1,600,000,000 ÷ $1,230,000,000 (FY2022 segment revenue) = **1.301x revenue**.
*Caveat: EV is the Aug-2023 announced figure; revenue is FY2022. A same-period revenue figure was not disclosed.*

**D-4 — Pye-Barker RMR growth, 2023 → 2025.**
$50,391,844 ÷ $15,300,000 = **3.294x** over two years.
Two-year CAGR = (3.294)^(1/2) − 1 = **81.5% per annum**.
*Overwhelmingly acquisition-driven — 57 acquisitions in 2025 alone. Do not read as organic.*

**D-5 — Pye-Barker implied EV range from the unconfirmed PE Hub report.**
17 × $350,000,000 = **$5,950,000,000**; 20 × $350,000,000 = **$7,000,000,000**.
*Consistent with "exceeding $6 billion." All inputs Press-derived and company-declined-comment; no transaction resulted.*

**D-6 — Pye-Barker implied EV / revenue on the same unconfirmed basis.**
$5,950,000,000 ÷ $2,000,000,000 = **2.98x**; $7,000,000,000 ÷ $2,000,000,000 = **3.50x**.
*Mismatched periods: the EBITDA and multiple are July-2024 reporting; revenue is FY2025. Directional only.*

**D-7 — Pye-Barker implied EBITDA margin on the unconfirmed figures.**
$350,000,000 ÷ $2,000,000,000 = **17.5%**.
*Cross-check: APi's Safety Services segment reported FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA margin of **15.9%** ($765m on $4,797m) — Disclosed. The 17.5% implied for Pye-Barker sits ≈1.6pp above the largest public comparable, which is plausible but not conservative.*

**D-8 — The RMR scale premium.**
46x − 36x = **10 turns of RMR**. 46 ÷ 36 = **1.278**, i.e. a **27.8% price uplift** for a seller above $500k RMR versus one below $50k RMR (2024 transactions, Barnes Associates).

**D-9 — Converting RMR multiples to revenue and gross-profit multiples.**
Inputs: Barnes RMR multiples (36x / 46x) and the Barnes Buchanan 2026 average monitoring/services margin of **51.2%**.
- Annualised monitoring revenue per $1 RMR = **$12.00**
- 46x RMR ÷ 12 = **3.83x annualised monitoring revenue**; 36x ÷ 12 = **3.00x**
- Annual gross profit per $1 RMR = $12.00 × 0.512 = **$6.144**
- 46 ÷ 6.144 = **7.49x annualised monitoring gross profit**; 36 ÷ 6.144 = **5.86x**
→ **These are EV / monitoring gross profit, NOT EV / EBITDA.** No selling, branch, G&A or corporate cost has been deducted. The equivalent EV/EBITDA is necessarily higher and is acquirer-specific. **Do not present D-9 outputs as EBITDA multiples.**

**D-10 — Cintas "All Other" segment growth (upper bound on fire protection).**
$1,146,018k ÷ $1,064,082k − 1 = **+7.70%** FY2025 vs FY2024.
Share of total company revenue FY2025 = $1,146,018k ÷ $10,340,181k = **11.08%**.
*"All Other" contains Fire Protection Services **and** Uniform Direct Sale. Fire Protection Services alone is **strictly smaller** than $1,146,018k and is **not** separately disclosed.*

**D-11 — APi tuck-in discipline versus the fire-platform aspiration.**
APi disclosed weighted-average bolt-on multiple **<6x** adj. EBITDA every year 2019–2024. Against the unconfirmed Pye-Barker platform range of 17–20x:
- 17 ÷ 6 = **2.83x**; 20 ÷ 6 = **3.33x**
→ **A platform is being valued at ≈2.8–3.3x the multiple its own tuck-ins are bought at.** That spread *is* the fire roll-up thesis. It is also the risk: it depends entirely on the platform multiple holding.

**D-12 — APi inspection leverage (Disclosed input, ratio restated).**
APi states ≈**"$3 – $4 of incremental service work generated annually"** per **$1** of inspection work, and **19 consecutive quarters of double-digit organic inspection revenue growth**. Implied service-to-inspection revenue leverage = **3.0x to 4.0x**.
