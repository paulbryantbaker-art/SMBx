# 12 — The ITM annuity, quantified

Run 2026-07-29. Buy-side stream. Object: put numbers on the claim that
inspection, testing and maintenance (ITM) is a legally mandated, recurring,
high-retention revenue stream that pulls through repair work — or establish
that it cannot be done from public sources.

**Verdict up front: PARTIALLY QUANTIFIED, and the partition is sharp.**

| Leg of the thesis | Status | Best public anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Pull-through ($1 inspection → $N service) | **Quantified** | APi CEO, on the record, twice: **$3–$4 of service per $1 of inspection**, plus three independent software-vendor datasets |
| Recurring share of revenue | **Quantified at the platform level** | APi **54%** of 2025 net revenue from inspection, service and monitoring; Chubb **>60%** recurring; ADT Commercial **≈30%** |
| Retention / attrition of an ITM book | **Partially quantified — one proxy only** | ADT Commercial **≈91% customer retention**, **≈10-year customer life** (Apr-2022 deck). No fire contractor discloses one |
| Contract term and structure | **Evidenced, not aggregated** | Three executed ITM contracts obtained, spanning 60-month auto-renew, 3-year + auto-renew, and 30-day-cancellable PO vehicle |
| Assignability on change of control | **Answered structurally, not statistically** | Depends entirely on whose paper. Contractor's own form is assignable; owner's and public-sector forms are not |
| Deficiency rate (the pull-through mechanism) | **Partially quantified** | Two municipal datapoints and a vendor claim, all conflicting; **no national deficiency rate exists** |
| Route density | **STILL UNQUANTIFIED from any credible source** | Only figure found is uncorroborated vendor marketing. Do not underwrite it |

## 1. Pull-through — this is now the best-evidenced leg of the thesis

### 1.1 The APi ratio is a company disclosure, not a third-party estimate

The prior stream carried this as a Speedwell attribution. It is not. It is
APi's own, stated on the record in at least two places.

**Basis: Disclosed.** APi Group Investor Day, slide 19, 2025-05-21:

> "$3 – $4 of incremental service work generated annually for every $1 of
> inspection work"

**Basis: Disclosed.** APi Group Q4 2025 earnings call, 2026-02-25, CEO Russell
A. Becker:

> "If you recall, we generate someplace between $3 and $4 worth of service work
> for every dollar of inspection revenue that we generate. And that continues to
> grow at that high-single-digit clip."

The clause after the ratio matters as much as the ratio. Becker is asserting the
pull-through multiple is **not decaying** — the derived service book compounds at
high single digits alongside the inspection base.

Population: APi Group consolidated Safety Services, predominantly commercial and
industrial fire and life safety in North America and Europe. It is **one
issuer's own management estimate** and no methodology is published. It should be
treated as the market's best-supported claim, not as an audited statistic.

The earlier and weaker form of the same claim, from APi's 2021 prospectus, shows
how much the disclosure has firmed up. **Basis: Disclosed.** APi Group Form
424B5, 2021-09-13:

> "Our go to market strategy is to sell inspection work first, because we
> estimate that every dollar sold can lead to subsequent service work."

"Can lead to" in 2021 became "$3–$4" by 2025.

### 1.2 The mix shift is disclosed and it is large

**Basis: Disclosed.** APi Q4 2025 earnings call, 2026-02-25, Becker:

> "We increased our percentage of revenue coming from inspections, service, and
> monitoring from 40% in 2021 to 54% in 2025."

**Basis: Disclosed.** APi Investor Day slide 55, 2025-05-21: "60%+ long-term net
revenues from inspection, service and monitoring revenues."

**Basis: Disclosed.** APi Form 424B5, 2021-09-13, on the pre-Chubb Life Safety
business:

> "We have grown the mix of service revenue as a proportion of net revenues for
> our Life Safety business from approximately 20% in 2008 to approximately 41%
> in 2020."

Two different series are in circulation and must not be blended: **Life Safety
segment service mix** (20% in 2008 → 41% in 2020) and **consolidated inspection
+ service + monitoring mix** (40% in 2021 → 54% in 2025). The denominators
differ.

**Basis: Disclosed.** APi Investor Day slide 17, 2025-05-21: "19 Consecutive
Quarters of Double-Digit Organic Inspection Revenues Growth." Updated on the
Q4 2025 call by CFO Glenn David Jackola to "over 20 quarters of this trend."

### 1.3 Independent vendor datasets — three of them, and they broadly agree

**ServiceTrade, Fire and Life Safety Benchmark Report 2024.** Basis: Disclosed
(vendor platform data).

> "For top performers, 19.5% of all work orders were pull-through in nature."

A second segment view of the same report renders the figure as **19.6%**. The
report is parameterised by the user's technician count, so the two numbers are
almost certainly different size cohorts of the same dataset. **Conflict
preserved, not averaged: 19.5% and 19.6%.** Neither is a whole-population figure
and no sample size is published.

Same report: top performers grew revenue **33.2%** YoY; completed **85.6%** of
jobs on time; invoiced within **1.6 days** (a second view of the report gives
1.7 days for top, 5.7 middle, 11.1 bottom).

**ServiceTrade, "5 Steps to Growing Pull-Through Repair Revenue."** Basis:
Disclosed (vendor platform data). This is the mechanism, decomposed:

- Top performers identified potential repairs on **≈25%** of work orders;
  bottom-half performers on **≈10%**.
- "Top performers consistently converted between **50% and 60%** of identified
  equipment issues into quotes for their customers. Bottom-half performers only
  quoted identified issues about **10%** of the time."
- Quote *approval* rates were similar between cohorts — "the difference in
  revenue came from volume rather than conversion quality."
- Fire protection specifically: "Top-half performers earned **38% more per work
  order** than bottom-half performers" and **99% more per customer**.

That last decomposition is the single most useful operating insight in this
file. **The pull-through gap is not a closing-skill gap; it is a
detect-and-quote gap.** A contractor who does not systematically record
deficiencies and does not systematically quote them leaves roughly half the
annuity's economics on the floor. That is a diligence-testable, post-close
fixable defect.

**ServiceTrade platform aggregate, 2025 full year.** Basis: Disclosed. Press
release 2026-02-05:

- "**$822 million in repair revenue tied to recurring inspections or maintenance
  work**"
- "$8 billion in invoices", "$9 billion in quotes", "$12 billion in proposals"
- "1.4 million quotes, achieving a **63% acceptance rate** with **40% of the
  quoted value approved**"
- 28 million billable hours; 6.1 million invoices; "over 1,300 fire protection
  and mechanical service contractors"

The 63%-by-count versus 40%-by-value split is worth flagging: **small quotes
convert; large ones do not.** The pull-through annuity is made of many small
compelled repairs, not a few large ones. That is consistent with APi's "≈$7K
Average Ticket Size in Safety Services: North America" (Investor Day slide 17).

**ServiceTitan, 2025 Commercial Service Market Report.** Basis: Disclosed
(survey). Published 2025-07-14; "over 1,000 commercial owners and executives
across the trades", fielded 2025-05-05 to 2025-05-23. Population is
MEP-weighted, **not** fire-specific — this matters and is the reason it is
carried separately.

- "**63% of contractors report that over half of their customer base is secured
  through PMAs**" (planned maintenance agreements)
- "**75% of contractors report they're getting over 20% of additional revenue
  from pull-through work**"

### 1.4 Where the vendor numbers land against each other

Three different measures of the same phenomenon, on three different bases:

| Measure | Figure | Basis of measurement | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pull-through as share of **work orders** | 19.5% / 19.6% (top performers) | count of work orders | ServiceTrade 2024 |
| Pull-through repair revenue as share of **invoiced dollars** | 10.3% (Estimated — see D10) | dollars | ServiceTrade 2025 aggregate |
| Contractors reporting **>20%** of revenue from pull-through | 75% of respondents | self-reported, MEP-weighted | ServiceTitan 2025 |
| Service dollars per inspection dollar | $3–$4 | dollars, one issuer | APi 2025/2026 |

These are not in conflict — they measure different things. But note the tension
worth carrying into an IC memo: **APi's $3–$4 implies service is 75–80% of the
inspection-plus-service block, while ServiceTrade's platform aggregate implies
pull-through repair is ≈10% of all invoiced dollars.** APi's ratio counts all
subsequent service work off an inspection relationship; ServiceTrade's $822M
counts only repair work explicitly tied to a recurring inspection or maintenance
job. The APi number is the wider definition and should be read as the ceiling.

## 2. The mandate is real, but capture of it is not

This is the finding that most changes the shape of the thesis.

**Basis: Disclosed.** APi Form 424B5, 2021-09-13:

> "Nearly all facilities that have existing life safety systems are required by
> law to have that system inspected on an annual basis."

**Basis: Disclosed.** APi/Chubb acquisition announcement, 2021-07-27:

> "50%+ of our revenue will be service based with meaningful,
> **statutorily-required**, recurring revenue"

So the legal duty is asserted by the largest filer in the industry. But the
municipal record shows the duty is **widely unenforced**, and that is the more
commercially interesting number.

**Basis: Disclosed (municipal).** City of Rockville, MD, 2024-01-31:

> "Of the 2,083 fire protection systems in the city, 1,313 (63%) are current on
> annual inspections."
> "Of the 2,083 systems, 1,933 (90%) were found compliant due to ongoing
> inspection and testing."
> "a 30% reduction in unwanted fire alarm and sprinkler activations citywide"

**Internal arithmetic conflict, preserved:** 1,933 ÷ 2,083 = 92.80%, not the
stated 90%. The 63% figure reconciles exactly (1,313 ÷ 2,083 = 63.03%). Use 63%
with confidence; treat the 90% as approximate and unreconciled.

**Read the 63% carefully. In a city that runs an active electronic
third-party-reporting programme — i.e. one of the most enforced jurisdictions in
the country — 37% of inspectable fire protection systems are not current on an
annual inspection.** The mandate is not self-executing. This cuts both ways for
a buyer: it caps the "everyone must buy this" story, and it is simultaneously
the largest identified pool of latent, legally-owed, uncaptured ITM demand in
any source reached.

**Basis: Press-derived (vendor claim, uncited).** BRYCER / The Compliance Engine
implementation plan, undated, hosted by City of Sedalia:

- "**40% of life safety systems go uninspected or maintained every year**"
- "32.5% of false alarms are due lack of maintenance and testing"
- "95% of AHJs do not have the resources to enforce their adopted fire code"
- "29% of fire code official's time is spent administering 3rd Party ITM reports"

The 40% claim is Brycer's, unsourced, and it is strikingly close to Rockville's
independently-measured 37%. Do not treat that as corroboration — Brycer's number
may well be the origin of the framing — but the two are consistent.

**Basis: Press-derived (vendor claim).** BRYCER presentation to Forney, TX,
council agenda packet:

- "**30-40% of all commercial buildings and multi-tenant inspectable buildings
  are in violation of code requirements**"
- "Less than 10% of fire departments can inspect all buildings within 12 months"
- "Less than 5% know about all life safety systems in their jurisdiction"
- Contractor filing fee: "**a per system, per address filing fee of $17**"
- "Contractors experience a **10.8x** difference in the cost of upload to the
  cost of increased work/revenue"

That 10.8x is Brycer selling contractors on the programme. It is the only
quantified statement anywhere of what an AHJ reporting mandate is worth to a
contractor. It is unaudited vendor marketing. **Do not underwrite it.**

**Basis: Press-derived (vendor case study, named jurisdiction).** BRYCER
"What is TCE", quoting Buncombe County, NC (TCE adopted July 2024):

> "we were finding **12-15% of the systems in our jurisdiction were out of
> compliance or had an outstanding deficiency**"
> "I now have a **2% or less** outstanding deficiencies"

**Conflict preserved.** Buncombe's 12–15% pre-programme non-compliance is far
below Brycer's own 30–40% headline and below Rockville's 37% not-current. The
three measure different things — outstanding deficiency, code violation, and
inspection currency are three distinct states — and the industry uses them
interchangeably. **A buyer diligencing a "deficiency rate" must specify which of
the three is meant.**

### 2.1 Programme reach and fee structure

**Basis: Disclosed.** BRYCER: "over **1,420** cities, counties, and fire
departments using our platform."

**Basis: Disclosed.** NFSA, 2018-12-05: "third-party reporting is utilized in at
least **37 states**." The same article concedes the evidence gap directly: "Some
of the jurisdictions have shown a significant increase in compliance with
NFPA 25 inspections and repairs since this reporting has been implemented" —
with no numbers behind it.

**Basis: Disclosed (municipal).** Seattle Fire Department: "**$35** filing fee
for submitting a report. One fee is charged per system type, per year, except
for hood systems, which are per every six-month service." Late fee of "**$12**
per report" since January 2019. "Reports must be submitted within **seven**
calendar days of the inspection or maintenance completion."

That fee schedule is a usable proxy for system-type density: Seattle bills per
system type per year, hoods twice a year — meaning a single building generates
multiple billable ITM events per annum, which is the structural reason ITM
revenue per site exceeds a naive "one annual inspection" model.

## 3. What "recurring" means in an actual contract — three forms, three answers

No filer discloses the composition of its ITM book. So this stream went to the
contracts themselves. Three executed or standard-form ITM agreements were
obtained in full. They are structurally incompatible with each other, and that
**is** the finding.

### 3.1 Contractor's own standard form — long, auto-renewing, escalating, assignable

**Fire Protection, Inc. (Seattle), Master Service Agreement.** Basis: Disclosed
(contractor's published standard form).

| Clause | Language |
|---|---|
| Term | "**60 months** commencing on the date of service activation" |
| Renewal | "shall renew automatically for successive **60 month** terms thereafter under the same terms and conditions" |
| Cancellation | "either party gives written notice of termination to the other at least **60 days** prior to the expiration of any term, extension, or renewal" |
| Escalation | "increase monthly, quarterly or annual rates … by an amount not to exceed the greater of **six percent (6%)** each year or the percent equal to the most recent 12-month percentage change in **CPI-U**" |
| Contractor assignment | "**FPI shall have the right to assign this Agreement** to a company licensed to perform the services and shall be relieved of any obligations" |
| Customer assignment | "Customer shall **not** be permitted to assign this Agreement without written consent of FPI" |
| Deficiencies | "If system devices or panels are deficient, FPI will provide Customer with a **written proposal for service and/or repairs**" |

This is the form a roll-up wants to buy. Five-year evergreen, contractual
6%-or-CPI escalator, unilateral assignment right in the contractor's favour, and
a written deficiency-to-proposal step baked into the contract — the pull-through
mechanism is a **contractual obligation of the contractor**, not a sales habit.

### 3.2 National-account form on the owner's terms — shorter, cancellable, NOT assignable

**Johnson Controls Fire Protection, LP — Physicians Realty L.P., contract
919272.** Basis: Disclosed (executed agreement, publicly posted).

| Clause | Language / value |
|---|---|
| Term | Commencement 2022-07-01, completion 2025-06-30 (**3 years**) |
| Renewal | "the term shall **automatically renew** for successive **one (1) year** periods if not terminated" |
| Termination | "may be terminated at any time by **either party** upon **forty-five (45) days'** prior written notice" |
| Contractor assignment | "**Contractor shall not assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of Owner**" |
| Scope | Fire alarm testing, sprinkler inspection, extinguisher testing, monitoring across multiple properties; annual inspections with semi-annual visuals and quarterly testing |
| Value | **$288,845** total — Y1 $87,576, Y2 $96,290, Y3 $104,979 |
| Billing | "monthly in advance, net 30" |

The escalator is in the price schedule rather than a formula clause: **+9.95%
year 1→2 and +9.03% year 2→3** (see D9). That is a real, executed, multi-year
ITM price escalation in a multi-site national account — materially above CPI over
the period, and evidence that ITM pricing power is genuine.

But note the two clauses that break the annuity story: **45-day termination for
convenience by either party**, and **no assignment without owner consent**. On a
three-year face term, a 45-day out means the economic term is 45 days.

### 3.3 Public-sector form — not a contract at all, in revenue terms

**Pye-Barker Fire & Safety — Lee County, FL, solicitation B210350NAT.** Basis:
Disclosed (awarded agreement).

| Clause | Language / value |
|---|---|
| Term | "one (1) three-year (3) period", renewals "not to exceed the initial Agreement term of three (3) years", "**Upon mutual written agreement of both parties**" |
| Termination | County may terminate "upon **thirty (30) days'** written notice" for "**any reason whatsoever**" |
| Escalation | none specified |
| Assignment | assignee must agree "in writing with the non-assigning party"; assignment of **payment claims** to financial institutions or a bankruptcy trustee permitted without County approval |
| Pricing | rate card **$80–$180/hour** by technician grade, **25%** materials markup |
| Volume | work "**on an as needed basis**" contingent on County purchase orders |

**This is a rate-card master agreement with zero volume commitment, cancellable
in 30 days for any reason.** It confers a right to be called, not a right to
revenue. Any diligence that counts a public-sector "3-year contract" as
contracted backlog is mis-stating the asset.

### 3.4 The assignability answer

The question posed — "is an ITM agreement assignable on a change of control?" —
has a clean structural answer and no statistical one.

**It depends entirely on whose paper the agreement is on.**

- **Contractor's own standard form → assignable by the contractor unilaterally**
  (FPI: "FPI shall have the right to assign this Agreement"). This is the
  typical small-contractor book that a roll-up buys.
- **National-account / institutional owner's negotiated form → not assignable
  without owner consent** (JCI–Physicians Realty). The larger and more
  attractive the logo, the more likely the contract is on the customer's paper
  and therefore consent-gated.
- **Public-sector award → consent-gated and volume-uncommitted** (Pye-Barker–Lee
  County).

The practical consequence: **an equity/stock purchase preserves the book; an
asset purchase puts the best-named accounts at risk of consent.** The perverse
diligence result is that the accounts a buyer most wants — multi-site national
logos — are precisely the ones least likely to travel in an asset deal.

**Not found, and it is the gap:** no source discloses what share of any
contractor's ITM book sits on each of the three forms. No filer, no rating
agency, no broker, no franchise document. **This remains the single most
commercially decisive unquantified variable in the industry.**

### 3.5 The filers' own language is internally contradictory, and it is still live

**Basis: Disclosed.** APi Form 424B5, 2021-09-13:

> "Maintenance and service revenues are less cyclical, and are **reasonably
> recurring due to consistent renewal rates** and deep customer relationships."

Against that, from the risk factors, **Basis: Disclosed**, APi Form DRS,
2019-12-23:

> "the fact that our **revenues are derived primarily from contracts with
> durations of less than six months** and the risk that customers will not renew
> or enter into new contracts"

The prior stream flagged this contradiction. **This stream establishes it is not
a stale 2019 artefact.** EDGAR full-text search on the exact phrase "durations
of less than six months" returns APi filings running through the **10-Q filed
2025-10-30**. The company simultaneously tells equity investors it has
"consistent renewal rates" and tells risk-factor readers its contracts run under
six months, and it has done so continuously for six years.

APi has **never** published a renewal rate, retention rate or attrition rate. It
has no S-1 (it came public via a UK-listed SPAC and a Form S-4); its **DRS of
2019-12-23 is the functional equivalent and it contains no retention metric** —
the financial and operational metric tables in that draft are redacted with `[●]`
placeholders.

### 3.6 Accounting treatment corroborates the short-duration reading

**Basis: Disclosed.** Cintas Form 10-Q, period ended 2025-08-31:

> approximately **95%** of revenue comes from "route servicing … performed by a
> Cintas employee-partner, at the customer's location of business"
> revenue recognised "over time as services are performed" and "**The Company's
> performance period generally corresponds with the monthly invoice period**"

A route-service business whose performance period is one month has, in
accounting terms, no long-dated contract asset at all. **"Recurring" in this
industry is a behavioural fact about customer habit and legal duty, not a
balance-sheet fact about contracted term.** Every source reached is consistent
with that reading.

**Basis: Disclosed.** APi Form 10-K, FY2025, filed 2026-02-25, risk factors:

> "our customers are **not contractually committed to procure specific volumes
> of services** under a contract. Revenue estimates reflected in our backlog can
> be subject to change due to a number of factors, including contract
> cancellations"

### 3.7 The one route-service comparator that does disclose a term

**Basis: Disclosed.** UniFirst Form 10-K, fiscal 2017:

> "We primarily serve our customers pursuant to **written service contracts that
> range in duration from three to five years**."

Population: uniform rental and cleaning, 300,000+ customer locations. This is a
labelled analogue, not a fire figure, and it is the only route-based recurring
service business found that discloses a contract term at all. It sets a useful
benchmark: **the adjacent, mature, publicly-traded route-service industry runs on
3–5 year written contracts.** Fire ITM's own standard forms (FPI's 60 months)
sit at or above that; its national-account forms (JCI's 45-day out) sit far
below.

## 4. Retention and attrition — one usable proxy, and it is better than expected

### 4.1 The commercial number nobody has been citing

**Basis: Disclosed.** ADT Inc., "Business Overview and Financial Modeling",
April 2022, page 10, slide "ADT Generates Attractive Returns Across Each
Segment":

> "**\~91% customer retention**"  *(Commercial segment)*

Same deck, page 9, Commercial segment: **"\~10 years"** customer life, and
**"\~30%"** recurring revenue as a percentage of segment total revenue.

**This is the most relevant retention figure in the public record for a
commercial fire and life safety book, and it has not been surfaced before in
this study.** Population: ADT Commercial — the commercial fire, life safety and
security business sold to GTCR in October 2023 and rebranded **Everon**. It is
not residential monitoring. It is the direct organisational ancestor of one of
the named platforms in the consolidator register.

Caveats, stated plainly:
- It is a marketing-deck figure with **no published definition** — logo
  retention, revenue retention and RMR retention are three different numbers and
  the deck does not say which. The `~` in ADT's original is reproduced here only
  inside the verbatim quotation.
- It is April 2022, pre-divestiture, and no successor figure has been published
  by Everon under GTCR ownership.
- It is a **blended** commercial security-plus-fire book, not a pure ITM book.

Even discounted for all three, ≈91% retention with a ≈10-year customer life is
a materially different animal from ADT's residential attrition and should be the
anchor a buy-side model uses for a commercial ITM book — clearly labelled as a
proxy.

### 4.2 The residential number, with its definition, so the two are never confused

**Basis: Disclosed.** ADT Inc. Q4/FY2025 results, 2026-03-02:

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Gross customer revenue attrition (TTM) | **13.1%** | 12.7% |
| Revenue payback (TTM) | **2.3 years** | 2.2 years |
| Total revenue | **$5,129M** | — |
| Monitoring and related services revenue | **$4,354M** | — |
| Security installation, product and other | **$775M** | — |
| End-of-period RMR (2025-12-31) | **$359M** | flat YoY |

**Basis: Disclosed.** ADT's own definition, from the April 2022 modelling deck:

> "Gross attrition is calculated on a trailing twelve-month basis, the numerator
> of which is the **RMR lost** during the period due to attrition, net of dealer
> chargebacks and reinstated customers, excluding contracts monitored but not
> owned and DIY customers, and the denominator of which is **total annualized RMR**
> based on an average of RMR under contract at the beginning of each month
> during the period."

**Population: residential and small-business monitoring subscribers. The
denominator is RMR, not revenue and not customers.** A 13.1% RMR-attrition rate
and a 91% commercial customer-retention rate are computed on different
numerators, different denominators and different populations. They must never be
placed on the same axis.

### 4.3 The denominator bug — rebuilt, and the recommendation

The prior stream flagged that ADT's 98.9% annualised-RMR-to-revenue uses
*monitoring* revenue as the denominator. Confirmed, and rebuilt (arithmetic at
D1):

| Denominator | Annualised RMR ÷ denominator | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and related services revenue ($4,354M) | $4,308M ÷ $4,354M | **98.9%** |
| **Total revenue ($5,129M)** | $4,308M ÷ $5,129M | **84.0%** |

**Recommendation: rebuild the whole ladder on total revenue.** Two reasons.
First, it is the only denominator every platform in the register reports.
Second, the monitoring-revenue denominator flatters ADT by ≈15 points and makes
the residential monitoring model look like a different species from an ITM
contractor when the actual gap is smaller.

On the total-revenue basis, the ladder's top rung is **ADT at 84.0%**, and the
Everon rung is independently corroborated by ADT's own segment disclosure at
**≈30% recurring revenue as a percentage of segment total revenue** (April 2022
deck, page 9) — within a point or two of the 32.1% the prior stream derived.
That is a genuine cross-check on the ladder's construction method.

**I could not re-verify the Pye-Barker 30.2%, ORR 8.9% or Security 101 6.1%
rungs in this stream** and will not restate them as verified here. The
prior stream's argument that **under ≈10%, "recurring" means inspection
contracts rather than assignable monitoring contracts** survives this stream's
evidence and is strengthened by §3.4: a ≈6–9% RMR-to-revenue business is
selling inspection agreements, and inspection agreements are the ones whose
assignability turns on whose paper they are written on.

### 4.4 What no fire contractor discloses

Restating the negative result precisely, because it is a finding:

**Not one fire protection contractor — filer or private, in any document reached
in this stream — discloses an ITM contract renewal rate, a customer retention
rate, or an attrition rate.** Checked: APi Group (10-K FY2025, DRS 2019, 424B5
2021, 424B2 2021, Investor Day 2025, Q4 2025 call), Cintas (10-K FY2024, FY2025,
10-Q Q1 FY2026), Johnson Controls, ADT, Rollins, UniFirst. EDGAR full-text
search for `"inspection, testing and maintenance"` on Form S-1 returns **eight
hits, none of them a fire or life safety business** (World Technology, Telx, Sun
Country Airlines, Xenith Bankshares, New Fortress Energy, Ormat). EDGAR
full-text for `"life safety" "retention rate"` on Form S-1 returns Alarm.com,
ClubCorp, Bright Horizons and Bally's Chicago — no fire contractor.

**There is no fire or life-safety-services S-1 in EDGAR.** The richest-source
route the brief identified does not exist to be mined.

### 4.5 Rating agency route — attempted and failed

Searched for rating actions and credit opinions on Pye-Barker (incl. Pye-Barker
Topco LLC), SFP Holding / Summit Fire, Sciens, Pavion, Convergint, Everon,
AI Fire, Marmic, Zeus Fire & Security, Encore Fire Protection. One S&P Global
Ratings article was located by title — *"Pye-Barker Topco LLC Rated 'B' On Debt
Refinancing"* — but the page returns only metadata and navigation markup with no
article body; the substantive credit opinion is behind entitlement.
`ratings.moodys.com` returns no indexable rating actions for these issuers.
Fitch has no public research on any of them.

**Conclusion on this route: private-platform operating metrics for US fire and
life safety are NOT publicly available through rating agency channels.** The
issuers are 144A private placements; the credit opinions exist but are
entitlement-gated. This is a paid-database task (S&P Capital IQ, Moody's

## 6. Cross-industry recurring-revenue comparators, on one basis

For calibrating what "recurring" is worth, all on **total revenue** as
denominator:

| Business | Recurring measure | Figure | Date | Population |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ADT Inc. | annualised RMR ÷ total revenue | **84.0%** (D1) | FY2025 | residential + small business monitoring |
| ADT Commercial (→ Everon) | recurring ÷ segment revenue | **≈30%** | Apr 2022 | commercial fire, life safety, security |
| APi Group | inspection + service + monitoring ÷ net revenue | **54%** | FY2025 | fire and life safety services, global |
| APi Group | same, target | **60%+** | long term | same |
| APi Life Safety segment | service ÷ net revenue | **41%** | 2020 | pre-Chubb Life Safety only |
| Chubb Fire & Security | "recurring in nature" | **"over 60%"** | pre-2021 | fire and security, 17 countries |
| Rollins | recurring services ÷ revenue | **≈75%** | FY2025 | pest control, route-based |
| Cintas | route-serviced ÷ revenue | **≈95%** | Q1 FY2026 | uniform, first aid, fire protection |

**Basis: Disclosed** for every row. Sources at §Sources.

Two observations a buyer should take from this table.

First, **APi at 54% and Chubb at "over 60%" bracket the ceiling for a
services-led fire platform**, and APi's own long-term target of 60%+ says
management believes the ceiling is near 60%. A target represented in diligence
as materially above 60% recurring is either defining recurring more loosely than
APi does, or is not comparable.

Second, **Rollins at ≈75% and Cintas at ≈95% show that route-based recurring
service businesses can run far higher recurring shares than fire ITM does.** The
gap is structural: fire platforms carry construction and retrofit revenue that
pest control and uniform rental do not. When a fire platform is valued off a
pest-control or uniform-rental recurring-revenue comp, that structural gap is
the thing being papered over.

## 7. Gross margin by work type — the reason ITM is defended

**Basis: Press-derived (investment bank sector report).** Meridian Capital,
*Fire & Life Safety Services M&A Market Update*, Winter 2025, authors Brian
Murphy and Aaron Franzheim:

| Work type | Gross margin |
|---|---|
| Testing and inspection (T&I) | **50%+** |
| Repair and maintenance (R&M) | **40%–50%** |
| Retrofit | **35%–45%+** |
| New construction | **25%–35%** |

Also: "Margins in excess of **20%** [EBITDA] indicative of strong labor and
operational efficiency"; "average of **38 transactions** announced per quarter
since 2020"; "Service revenue is generally characterized as recurring/reoccurring
with strong margins."

**This is the economic core of the annuity and it had not been quantified in
this study.** T&I at 50%+ gross margin against new construction at 25–35% means
the mix shift APi discloses — 40% to 54% of revenue from inspection, service and
monitoring between 2021 and 2025 — is worth roughly 15–20 points of gross margin
on the migrated revenue. That, not the recurrence per se, is what the buyer is
paying the premium for.

Meridian's report contains **no** recurring-revenue percentage, no retention
rate, no contract term, no pull-through ratio and no RMR or EBITDA multiple —
checked directly. The sell-side does not publish those either.

## Derivations

**D1 — ADT annualised-RMR-to-revenue, both denominators, FY2025.**
End-of-period RMR at 2025-12-31 = $359M (Disclosed).
Annualised RMR = $359M × 12 = **$4,308M**.
Against monitoring and related services revenue: $4,308M ÷ $4,354M = 0.98944 → **98.9%**.
Against total revenue: $4,308M ÷ $5,129M = 0.83993 → **84.0%**.
Difference = 14.9 percentage points. `Basis: Estimated` (arithmetic on Disclosed inputs).

**D2 — Cintas Fire Protection Services share of total revenue, FY2025.**
$817.463M ÷ 7.9% (disclosed share) implies total of $10,347M, consistent with
the disclosed "$10.3 billion". Share carried as the **disclosed 7.9%**, not
recomputed. `Basis: Disclosed`.

**D3 — Cintas revenue per local delivery route, FY2025.**
Total revenue $10.3B × 95% route-serviced = $9,785M.
$9,785M ÷ 12,100 routes = **$808,678 per route**, stated as **≈$809K**.
`Basis: Estimated`. **Caveat: whole-company. Cintas does not disclose routes by
business line. This is not a fire protection route figure.**

**D4 — Cintas customers per route, FY2025.**
"over one million businesses" ÷ 12,100 routes = **≈83 customers per route**.
`Basis: Estimated`. Caveat: a customer taking uniform rental, first aid and fire
protection may be served by more than one route, so 83 is a lower bound on
stops-per-route-year and tells nothing about stops per day.

**D5 — Everon locations and technicians per branch, 2023-10-02.**
300,000 customer locations ÷ 2,300 technicians = **130.4 locations/technician**.
300,000 ÷ 100 branches = **3,000 locations/branch**.
2,300 ÷ 100 = **23 technicians/branch**.
`Basis: Estimated`. Caveat: "more than" and "over" qualifiers on all inputs make
these upper bounds on the denominators and lower bounds on the ratios;
monitoring-only sites inflate the location count.

**D6 — APi North America team members per branch, 2024.**
≈10,500 team members ÷ 124 branches = **84.7 per branch**.
`Basis: Estimated`. Caveat: team members, not technicians; branch-attached
headcount only.

**D7 — APi implied inspection revenue share, FY2025.**
Given inspection + service + monitoring = 54% of net revenue (Disclosed), and
service = 3× to 4× inspection (Disclosed), and letting monitoring = M ≥ 0:
at 3×, inspection = (54% − M) ÷ 4 ≤ **13.5%**;
at 4×, inspection = (54% − M) ÷ 5 ≤ **10.8%**.
So **inspection revenue is at most ≈11% to ≈13.5% of APi net revenue**, and
lower to the extent monitoring is non-trivial.
`Basis: Estimated`. **This is an upper-bound band, not a point estimate.** No
dollar figure is asserted because APi's FY2025 net revenue was not independently
confirmed in this stream.

**D8 — Rockville ITM capture and compliance, 2024-01-31.**
Systems current on annual inspection: 1,313 ÷ 2,083 = **63.03%** → **36.97% not
current**.
Systems found compliant: 1,933 ÷ 2,083 = **92.80%**, against the city's stated
**90%**. **Conflict preserved; the city's own arithmetic does not reconcile on
the second figure.** `Basis: Estimated` (arithmetic on Disclosed counts).

**D9 — JCI–Physicians Realty contract escalation, 2022–2025.**
Y1 $87,576 → Y2 $96,290: +$8,714 = **+9.95%**.
Y2 $96,290 → Y3 $104,979: +$8,689 = **+9.03%**.
Two-year CAGR: (104,979 ÷ 87,576)^(1/2) − 1 = **+9.49%**.
Sum check: 87,576 + 96,290 + 104,979 = **$288,845** ✓ matches stated total.
`Basis: Estimated` (arithmetic on Disclosed contract values).

**D10 — ServiceTrade pull-through repair revenue as share of invoiced dollars, FY2025.**
$822M repair revenue tied to recurring inspections or maintenance ÷ $8,000M
invoices = **10.3%**.
`Basis: Estimated`. **Caveat: the denominator spans fire protection AND
mechanical contractors on the platform; the numerator is pull-through repair
only. Not a fire-only ratio, and not comparable to APi's $3–$4 which uses a
narrower denominator.**

**D11 — ServiceTrade quote size skew, FY2025.**
63% of quotes accepted by count; 40% of quoted value approved. Implies the mean
accepted quote is smaller than the mean issued quote by a factor of
(0.40 ÷ 0.63) = **0.635**. `Basis: Estimated`. Reading: pull-through revenue is
composed of many small compelled repairs, not few large ones.

**D12 — Cintas Fire Protection Services growth.**
Q1 FY2026 $221.900M ÷ Q1 FY2025 $197.497M − 1 = **+12.36%**.
FY2025 $817.463M against FY2024 ≈$728M = **≈+12.3%** (FY2024 figure read from a
truncated table render; treat the annual growth as approximate, the quarterly as
exact). `Basis: Estimated`.

**D13 — Cintas route productivity change, FY2024 → FY2025.**
Routes 11,700 → 12,100 = **+3.42%**; revenue +7.7% (Disclosed).
Implied revenue per route growth = 1.077 ÷ 1.0342 − 1 = **+4.14%**.
`Basis: Estimated`. Whole-company, not fire-specific.
