Key industry verticals

Buy-side M&A for acquirers of private companies under $250M in revenue.

Retainer plus success fee, paid by the acquirer — never the seller. We work a handful of lanes, chosen deliberately, and we decline the rest.

Fire & life safety
Revenue that renews because the code says so.
Sprinkler and alarm inspection, testing and maintenance under NFPA 25 and 72. Suppression, detection, monitoring. Every installed system becomes an inspection obligation — and an annuity.
The operator base is deep, independent, and aging. Most of these owners are closer to retirement than to a succession plan.
Who we run it for
Family offices, independent sponsors, and first-time platforms that want non-discretionary recurring revenue.
Why this lane
Code-mandated recurring revenue with a long independent tail. Consolidation here is far from finished.
Elevator & escalator service
The compliance annuity with the longest independent tail.
Maintenance, repair, and modernization under mandated safety inspection. Contract books renew year after year, capex stays light, and independents still carry a large share of the installed base.
Who we run it for
Buyers who want annuity economics at service-company scale — sponsors, family offices, first-time platforms.
Why this lane
Mandated inspections, sticky contracts, aging owners. The earliest-stage lane we cover.
Power & grid infrastructure services
The grid runs on founder-owned shops.
Transformer repair and refurbishment, substation construction and maintenance, certified electrical testing. Electrification runs on this layer — and most of the companies doing the work are certified, founder-owned, and hard to replicate.
Who we run it for
Sponsors and family offices entering the power theme at service-company scale.
Why this lane
Structural demand, certification gates, and owners who built these firms a generation ago.
Building automation & critical power services
Recurring service where downtime isn't an option.
Building automation and controls, commissioning, critical power and cooling service. Data centers and healthcare keep the demand structural — and the service contracts recurring.
Who we run it for
Sponsors chasing data-center-adjacent services, and operator-acquirers from the mechanical world.
Why this lane
Recurring service revenue and technical crews, in demand that is structural rather than cyclical.
Testing, inspection & certification / NDT
Certification is the gate.
Nondestructive testingCode inspectionMaterials testing labs
Regional specialist labs and field-inspection firms: nondestructive testing, code inspection, materials testing. Demand is written into code, not into budgets.
The certified workforce is aging faster than it's being replaced. Succession is the story in almost every shop.
Who we run it for
Sponsors seeking certification moats.
Why this lane
Code-driven demand behind a workforce moat competitors can't hire their way through.
Environmental & industrial cleaning services
Permits gate entry.
Permitted industrial and environmental services, remediation, industrial cleaning. RCRA authorizations and state operator certifications keep casual buyers out.
Demand is regulation-driven, the businesses rarely reach a broker, and the permit transfer itself is a project — one we plan for from the first call.
Who we run it for
Sponsors and emerging platforms working below the strategic tier.
Why this lane
Regulation-driven revenue behind permit gates most buyers can't underwrite.
Water & wastewater contract O&M
Revenue as durable as the water bill.
Contract operations for municipal and industrial water and wastewater systems. Multi-year contracts, non-discretionary demand, and systems aging faster than the towns can staff them.
Who we run it for
Buyers who want the most durable revenue in the services economy — and can be patient with municipal timelines.
Why this lane
Mission-critical recurring contracts that consolidation has barely touched.
Specialty & MRO distribution
Authorization is the moat.
Niche industrial product lines, vendor-authorized distribution, VMI and integrated supply. The revenue behaves like a contract — and the authorizations don't transfer casually, which is exactly the diligence we run.
Who we run it for
Distribution platforms and holdcos compounding niche product lines that don't compete with each other.
Why this lane
Vendor-authorization moats across many non-competing product-line lanes.
Machine shops & precision manufacturing
Qualification makes revenue stick.
Certified machine shops and precision manufacturers — AS9100, ISO 13485, defense-qualified. Reshoring is pulling work back to shops that spent years earning their qualifications; those cycles make revenue durable and the businesses hard to value from the outside.
Who we run it for
Manufacturing-focused sponsors, family offices, and operator-acquirers.
Why this lane
The earliest-stage manufacturing lane, with tailwinds that are policy, not fashion.
Food contract manufacturing & co-packing
Sticky programs, signed for years.
Contract manufacturing and co-packing for food brands, held together by multi-year supply agreements and qualification runs. Fragmented nationally — with genuine density in our own backyard.
Who we run it for
Sponsors and strategics buying capacity, and holdcos collecting sticky supply agreements.
Why this lane
Recurring supply agreements, plus regional concentration where we sit.
Non-emergency medical transport
Recurring trips, funded by reimbursement.
Scheduled, recurring, Medicaid- and Medicare-funded transport. Fragmented and early — with payor and reimbursement diligence treated as the deal, not a footnote.
Who we run it for
Sponsors and holdcos comfortable with reimbursement-funded revenue.
Why this lane
Recurring demand and an open runway, entered with eyes open.
Revenue cycle management & medical billing
Fragmented, clean to diligence, and changing fast.
Many owners, clean books, active consolidation — and automation reshaping the work, which is why we underwrite the niche before the number.
Who we run it for
Buyers selective about which billing niches hold as automation compresses the routine work.
Why this lane
Fragmentation and fee-friendly deal sizes — in the niches that hold.
Home & commercial services
Where the record was built.
Residential & commercial services tradesCommercial landscaping — contract-heavy only
Thirty-six acquisitions across the residential and commercial services trades. I was inside the consolidation wave: what a platform pays for, what dies in diligence, what integration actually costs.
Much of that world is consolidated now. We take mandates here selectively — where a genuine lane is still open, like contract-heavy commercial landscaping — and decline them where the trade is saturated. The mechanics haven't changed. The current focus applies them one wave earlier.
