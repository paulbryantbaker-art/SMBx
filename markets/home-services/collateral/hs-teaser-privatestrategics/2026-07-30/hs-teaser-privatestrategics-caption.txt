Private strategics bought 34 HVAC businesses this year. Last year they bought 45.

The buyer who used to outbid you on the shop two towns over is leaving the market.

Capstone Partners' HVAC Services M&A Update, published 27 July, counts 92 announced or completed transactions year to date, down 4.2% on the same period last year. Capstone reads its own data as steady — "YTD transactional activity remains steady in the HVAC Services space." The movement is underneath the total.

Private strategics: 45 to 34. Down eleven deals, the largest single change in the set.
Public strategics: 5 to 11. More than doubled.
Sponsor add-ons: 36 to 38.
New platform creations: 10 to 9.

Read those four lines together and the shape of the market changes. Sponsors are not opening new fronts, they are thickening the ones they already hold. The listed strategics have stepped up hard. And the buyer who historically set the floor price on a small local deal — the contractor two towns over, buying the business he has known for twenty years — has pulled back further than anyone else in the data.

For a sponsor or an operator trying to assemble a first platform rather than sell into someone else's, that is the whole read: competition for the unassembled metro is thinner than a flat deal count suggests.

One caution before anyone builds on it. This is Capstone's tracked universe, not a census, and their December update counted 149 transactions to a different cutoff — it does not chain to the 92. Treat the direction as the finding, not the level.

I spent last week assembling the full picture: six trades sized separately, the platform map with sponsors and operating brands, unit economics, and the metros genuinely still open.

If you buy or run home-services businesses — which of those four lines matches your own pipeline? I am most interested in whether the private-strategic pullback is real on the ground, or an artifact of what gets announced.

Figures: Capstone Partners, HVAC Services M&A Update, 27 July 2026.
