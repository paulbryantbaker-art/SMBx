# DFW — platform-owned locations, from each platform's own site

**What this pass did.** Took the 34 parents in the consolidator register and
checked each one's own website — brand roster, locations page, or the `About`
boilerplate of a dated press release — for an operating location inside the
Dallas–Fort Worth–Arlington MSA as delineated by OMB Bulletin 23-01 (Collin,
Dallas, Denton, Ellis, Hunt, Johnson, Kaufman, Parker, Rockwall, Tarrant, Wise).

**What it did not do.** It did not count locations from a directory, a search
engine listing, or an aggregator. Nothing here is a Places result. Every entry
below traces to the owner's own publication, which is the standard a name has to
meet before it reaches a client document.

**Standing limit.** Absence of a DFW brand on a parent's site is *not* evidence
the parent has no DFW location. Two parents publish no brand roster at all. Where
that is the case it is recorded as **not confirmed**, not as zero.

## 4. Parents whose DFW position could NOT be established

**Apex Service Partners.** The master places Apex in DFW. Apex's own site
publishes no brand roster — 75 brands are claimed in its May 2026 release, 11 are
identifiable from any source in the research, and none of the 11 is a DFW
nameplate. **This is the largest single hole in the subtraction.** Any independent
residual computed today is overstated by whatever Apex owns in the metro, and
that quantity is unknown rather than zero.

**Franchise systems — Neighborly (KKR) and Authority Brands (Apax + BCI).** Both
operate franchise territories across DFW under Aire Serv, Mr. Rooter, Mr.
Electric, One Hour Heating & Air, Benjamin Franklin Plumbing and Mister Sparky.
**A franchisee is independently owned** and is acquirable, subject to its
franchise agreement. So these locations are neither platform-owned nor plainly
independent, and they need their own line in any banding. Franchisee counts by
metro are not published on either franchisor's site.

**ServiceMaster Brands** carries no HVAC, plumbing or electrical brand at all —
its own portfolio is restoration, cleaning and moving. The master lists it among
franchise platforms; it should not be treated as an HVAC consolidator.

## 5. What this changes about the master's DFW row

`master.md` §5.1 records the DFW row as: *"Wrench (Berkeys, Baker Brothers), SEER
(Swan), Apex, Roto-Rooter (Fort Worth) — Saturated"*, four platforms, against a
closing line of *"4–5+ platforms"* for the saturated metros.

Counting the enumerated tables above: **ten residential-side parents and eight
commercial-mechanical parents hold a confirmed DFW location**, and one more
(Apex) is unresolved. On the residential side alone that is more than twice the
master's count, and the commercial layer is absent from the master's map
entirely.

Two things follow, and they point in opposite directions:

**The saturation read gets stronger on presence.** More parents hold ground in
this metro than the master records, two of them are headquartered in it, and the
most recent entries — United Building Solutions in January 2026, Roto-Rooter's
Fort Worth franchise buy-back in March 2026 — are this year.

**But presence is not share, and that is the whole question.** Ten to eighteen
parents holding perhaps twenty-odd named locations tells you nothing about what
fraction of the metro's HVAC establishments they represent, and still less about
what fraction of the *buyable* slice. Twenty locations against a few thousand
establishments is a small number; against the several-hundred-establishment band
that is actually acquirable it could be a large one. **The denominator is the
finding, and it is not in this file.** It needs the Census establishment-size
distribution, which is coverage row 2 and is currently blocked.

So this pass sharpens the numerator and leaves the headline question open. It
does not confirm the master's *Saturated* label and it does not overturn it.

## 6. The 238220 problem, stated once, explicitly

Every count above will eventually be set against a Census establishment count
under **NAICS 238220 — Plumbing, Heating, and Air-Conditioning Contractors**.
That single code bundles three distinctions a residential HVAC thesis depends on:

1. **HVAC and plumbing together.** Several parents above are plumbing-only
   (P3/Schrader) or repiping-only (Repipe Specialists). They belong in a 238220
   denominator and not in an HVAC one.
2. **Residential and commercial together.** The eight parents in §2 are
   commercial-mechanical. They sit inside 238220 and outside a residential
   thesis, and there is no way to net them out of a county establishment count
   without an assumption.
3. **Service and new construction together.** The recurring-revenue logic the
   whole thesis rests on belongs to service and replacement work. Comfort Systems
   reports 63.2% of its own revenue as new-construction installation — inside the
   same code.

Where the 2022 Economic Census publishes class-of-customer or product-line detail
for 238220, that detail resolves some of this. Where it does not, the split has
to be carried as a stated assumption and labelled as one. **This is the soft spot
of the entire build.** It is flagged here once, per house standard, and any
figure downstream that silently treats 238220 as "residential HVAC" is wrong.

## 7. Establishments are not companies

Every parent above holds its DFW position through operating locations, and CBP
counts locations. Wrench alone shows two nameplates; ARS shows two; Roto-Rooter
two; Legacy two. A subtraction done in establishments and a subtraction done in
companies give different answers, and the target count is a company count. The
multi-unit share in CBP is the handle, and it is coverage row 5.

## What we don't know yet

- **What Apex owns in DFW.** Unresolved, and it is the biggest single gap.
- **How many franchisee-operated HVAC and plumbing businesses trade in the metro
  under Neighborly and Authority Brands nameplates.** Not published by either.
- **The denominator.** Establishment counts and their size distribution for the
  eleven counties — blocked on Census API access.
- **Location counts per nameplate.** A named brand may run one location or six;
  none of these parents publishes a per-brand branch count.
- **Whether any of these DFW locations has changed hands since its parent's page
  was last updated.** Rosters lag closings, which is the same reason the master
  carries Wrench at 17/25/28 brands.
