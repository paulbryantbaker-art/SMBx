## WHY THIS CHANGED

THE LINE v1 was written to make a **software product** a regulatory safe harbor and trust moat: *"smbX never takes transaction-based, success, or contingent compensation — all revenue is software work only."* That intent was correct for a product being sold into an open market.

That market is gone. AI labs — Claude Finance, Perplexity Finance, and others — have saturated the pure-software deal-analysis lane. Selling smbX.ai as a self-serve product is no longer differentiated.

**The pivot:** smbX.ai is no longer a product. It is a **shop** — an AI-forward, buy-side corp-dev-as-a-service practice. One practitioner (Paul), lean and fast, running acquisitions for buyers through close. DEFINITIVE and Yulia are the instruments that let one person do the work of a team.

THE LINE v2 is therefore no longer a *software* safe harbor. It is the **practitioner's perimeter** — the ethical and regulatory boundary that keeps the practice defensible. Its old "no success fee ever" absolute was built for a product and does not survive contact with real buy-side execution. This version states precisely what the practice forbids and what it permits.

## THE LINE — WHAT WE WILL AND WILL NOT DO

### FORBIDDEN (the perimeter)

1. **No sell-side representation.** We never represent a seller, never work a seller's outcome for compensation, never take a listing. Not for a retainer, not for a success fee, not ever.
2. **No two-sided engagement.** We never sit between a buyer and a seller. We never take compensation from both sides of a deal, and never take compensation from a transaction as a neutral intermediary.
3. **No representation of a buyer we do not exclusively serve on a given target.** One buyer per target. We never run the same target for two competing clients.
4. **No deals above the eligibility ceiling.** Targets at or above $250M revenue are outside the practice.
5. **No advice we are not qualified or licensed to give.** Where specialized licensed expertise is required (securities, tax opinions, legal opinions, formal appraisal), we coordinate the specialist — we do not substitute for them.

### PERMITTED (how a real buy-side practice works)

1. **Buy-side retainer.** A monthly or engagement retainer for corp-dev-as-a-service work, paid by our client the acquirer.
2. **Buy-side success fee.** A success fee on closing, **paid by our own client the acquirer, out of the acquirer's pocket, for work we did for the acquirer.** This is how independent sponsors and buy-side advisors are paid. It does not touch the sell side and does not extract money from the transaction as a neutral.
3. **Running the deal to close for the buyer** — negotiation, term-shaping, and driving to signing, on behalf of and at the direction of our client.
4. **Using DEFINITIVE and Yulia as the practice's instruments** — the AI produces the analysis, documents, models, and coordination; the practitioner directs it and owns the client relationship.

## WHY THIS PERIMETER IS DEFENSIBLE

The regulatory and trust danger THE LINE was always aimed at is the **two-sided, sell-side, or neutral-intermediary** fee — the structure where an advisor extracts contingent money from a transaction while owing duties to more than one party. THE LINE v2 forbids all of that absolutely.

A **buy-side-only** retainer-plus-success-fee, paid by your own client the acquirer for work done for the acquirer, is a different animal — it is standard buy-side advisory and independent-sponsor economics. Principals and their retained buy-side advisors acquiring private companies were never the broker-registration problem that two-sided brokering is.

**Standard: make any accusation implausible.** We operate conservatively inside the perimeter, not hard against it. The buy-side-only commitment, the one-buyer-per-target rule, and the sell-side prohibition are what make a "you played both sides" accusation structurally impossible.

## THE ONE THING TO CONFIRM WITH COUNSEL (once)

Moving from "corp-dev function that stops at the counterparty boundary" to "practitioner who runs the negotiation to close for a success fee" is the exact activity the securities and state M&A-broker laws care about. This is the single place in the model not to rely on gut. Confirm once, then put it to bed:

1. **M&A Broker Exemption (Exchange Act §15(b)(13)):** Does the buy-side-success-fee practice, as structured, sit inside the federal exemption? Confirm the eligibility conditions (private-company targets, size thresholds, no public offering, buyer control-and-active-management post-close, no dual representation) against the actual engagement shape.
2. **State M&A-broker / business-broker registration:** Several states have their own regimes that do not perfectly track the federal exemption. Confirm registration status in the states where the practice will operate.
3. **Engagement-letter language:** Have counsel confirm the buy-side-only representation, the success-fee trigger, and the sell-side prohibition are papered cleanly.

This is a one-time, tightly-scoped counsel question — cheap insurance for the perimeter the entire practice rests on.
