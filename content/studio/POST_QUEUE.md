**What the Sunday run reads and writes.** A scheduled Cowork session takes the
top five unposted angles, drafts them against the weekly spine, files the drafts
in this project, and marks them `drafted` here. Paul reviews once. He posts.

Companion to `claude/STUDIO_ROTATION.md` (markets) and

## Why weekly and not daily

Five separate daily runs produce five disconnected posts and five interruptions.
One weekly run produces a week that argues in sequence — Monday's claim sets up
Thursday's objection — and costs one review sitting. **Posting stays daily.**

## THE THREE LAWS OF A DRAFT

**1 · The evidence grade decides the sentence.**

The substrate grades every problem STRONG / MODERATE / THIN. That grade is not
decoration:

- **STRONG** — the post may state the figure as fact, with its publisher named.
- **MODERATE** — state it with the range and the reason it varies.
- **THIN** — **the post makes an argument, not a claim.** No number.

A draft that states a THIN figure as fact is a failed draft, however well it
reads. The `$10 trillion` boomer headline is THIN and the substrate says so.

**2 · Source interest is disclosed inline, in the post.**

SPS is owned by Bain and With Intelligence and sells origination analytics.
Axial sells access to the channel it measures. QoE providers publishing
broken-deal costs sell QoE. **Say so in the post.** For this audience that is
not a hedge — it is the differentiator, and it is the thing a competitor
posting the same statistic will not do.

**3 · Nothing ships that carries a retired figure.**

Run `retired-check` against every draft before it reaches Paul. A caption is the
most exposed artifact this practice produces and was, until 2026-08-10, the
least guarded — the 40% recurring-revenue threshold went out on 8 August, two
days after `A.0.6` retired it, because that sweep checked specs and PDFs and a
caption is neither.

**And publication is Paul's, always.** The run drafts. It never posts.

## What is NOT in the queue, and why

- Anything on the **retired list** for a market. `retired-check` enforces it.
- **Fee talk, pricing, sell-side framing.** THE LINE.
- **Valuation on a named target.** Ever.
- Any figure from the substrate's *"could not verify"* list — the $10T headline,
  "one-third of LOIs never close", the 40–50% SBA decline, the ~250,000-company
  universe, the claim that pre-LOI screening reduces break rates. That last one
  is the tempting one, because it is the whole pitch. **No causal study exists.
  Argue it; do not claim it.**

## What the app should track

This file is the content guide. The app is the tracker. One row per angle.

**Source of record:** this file, in `content/studio/POST_QUEUE.md`. The app
mirrors it; it does not own it. A weekly Cowork run marks angles `drafted` here
and writes the drafts to the project. Edit here, sync to the app — not the
reverse.

| Column | Type | From |

| `draft_path` | TEXT | `claude/POSTS_WEEK_<date>.md` in the project |
| `collateral_path` | TEXT | `markets/<m>/collateral/<slug>/<date>/` where a post has a carousel |
| `retired_check` | TEXT | `clean` · `flagged` · `not_run` — never blank |

**Two things the tracker must not do.**

1. **It must not fire anything.** Migration `122_stop_research_schedules.sql`
   disabled the scheduler after a saved campaign ran unattended on the metered
   key. A posting calendar is *data* — rows with dates and statuses. It needs no
   dispatcher and must not re-arm one. Leave `RESEARCH_SCHEDULES_ENABLED` unset.

2. **It must not mark anything `posted` automatically.** Publication is Paul's
   decision. `posted` is set by a human action in the UI, never inferred.

**Closing the loop.** `server/services/linkedinAnalytics.ts` already ingests
Paul's LinkedIn export mechanically — every number in the app is a number
LinkedIn wrote. Joining that to `queue_id` gives per-angle and per-format
performance, which is what the next week's drafting run should read.
