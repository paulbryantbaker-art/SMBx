## 0 · Rules that govern every day

- One post per 24 hours, maximum. No links — in body or first comment. 0 hashtags.
- Every source named in the post, with its commercial interest where it has one. That disclosure is the differentiator. Nobody else in the feed does it.
- Attribution, always: "led or co-led in the course of employment," "~150 acquisitions over twenty years," "a decade running corporate development inside a home-services consolidator." **Never employer names in posts. Never implied smbX engagements.**
- Track record numbers stay approximate: "about 150," "roughly 36."
- The 90-minute protocol on every post: stay, reply substantively, leave 5–10 real comments on the named DFW dealmaker list.
- **[RECEIPT] slots are Paul's.** Seven posts are gated on the receipts inventory. An invented deal detail is a practice-ending error. If a receipt isn't ready, the post slides; the number posts around it hold the cadence.
- Video days: real camera. Not HeyGen — an AI avatar in front of this audience undercuts the exact authority the content is building. HeyGen stays platform-side.
- Number posts run as single image + short text (under-5K-follower reach logic). Save carousels for after the account has distribution.

**Measurement:** non-follower reach %, profile views from target firms, DMs and meeting
asks, saves. Not likes. Check follower vs non-follower reach at Day 14.

## A.2 · Findings from the retired-figure register, before any of this drafts

Run before transcription, against `studio/markets/*/retired.md` — the practice's own
denylist of figures that were carried, then killed by a named correction pass. Three
findings, and the first one is a stop.

**⛔ Day 11 · "PE add-ons up 88%" is a RETIRED figure — R-HS-017.** The register's
verdict: *"RETIRED, NOT FOUND in any S&P or secondary source, and contradicted by
Capstone's actual count of 38 add-ons."* The plan credits the number to Capstone, and
Capstone's own count is what contradicts it. **This is the exact failure the register
exists to catch and the figure does not ship.**

**And the practice has already written the correction, which is where the replacement
copy comes from.** The 88% entered this material on page 6 of the P-5 carousel — *"PE
completed 32 HVAC add-on transactions globally through early June 2025, up 88% from 17
a year earlier (S&P Global, citing Capstone)"* — and P-5's own rendered caption retires
it in public: *"I had a figure staged for this post claiming PE add-ons had nearly
doubled year over year, attributed to S&P. I could not stand it up at that source or
any other. Capstone's actual count is 38 add-ons against 36 — two deals — and sector
volume is down 4.2%."* Day 11 is the pre-correction page 6, restated. **Rebuild the
hook on the corrected numbers or on the deal count and entry multiple alone** — the
"more deals, lower entry" claim survives without the 88%.

**⚠ Day 11 · the two Capstone volume claims point opposite ways.** The plan's hook says
"149 HVAC services deals closed in 2025, up 12.9%"; P-5's corrected caption says
"sector volume is down 4.2%" — both credited to Capstone. They are almost certainly
different series over different windows (the transaction count versus sector volume),
and that is exactly why one post cannot carry either without naming its basis and its
window. Reconcile against Capstone directly before drafting.

**Day 11 and Day 19 · the multiples need their windows.** P-5 states them with the
periods they span: *"Capstone puts HVAC services at 9.5x EV/EBITDA across 2024–YTD 2026
against 13.3x across 2021–23. Roughly four turns."* Carry the windows; "down from a
13.3x peak" without them invites the reader to date the peak wherever they like.

**⚠ Days 17 and 23 · the $1.2T buyout figure carries a vintage.** R-HS-008/009 retired
`$1.2 trillion` only where it was used as *sector-segmented* dry powder — the plan's use
(Bain's all-sector global buyout figure, universe named) is the corrected use and is not
a hit. But the same verdict records that **Bain's 2026 edition has moved the figure to
$1.3T**. On a post whose entire argument is universe-and-vintage discipline, quoting the
2025 number without its year would undercut itself. State the year, or state both.

**⚠ Day 21 · the fire-and-life-safety counts are contested inside this practice.**
R-FS-024 corrected a "110 transactions" claim to 133 across two Meridian editions, with
the standing warning: *"Deal tempo is edition-specific and the two editions differ; do
not merge them into a single claim."* The plan's 129 / 109 are Lincoln International's,
a different publisher — no denylist hit — but the same merge risk applies, and the day
also stacks "my own tracking" on top. Keep the two counts attributed separately.

**One near-collision, noted so nobody merges them later:** `13.3x` appears in this
practice twice — as the HVAC entry-multiple peak (Day 11 / Day 19, Capstone) and as the
Chubb carve-out multiple (R-FS-038, `PRE-2023`, including synergies, *"may not appear on
a chart with"* a post-2022 multiple). Different claims. Never the same axis.

**And one structural conflict, which is Paul's call, not a finding:** Day 14 posts on
DealSource day. The Aug 18 calendar made Sept 3 a deliberate **blackout** on the
reasoning that the 90-minute protocol cannot be honoured from a conference floor — and
§0 above still binds that protocol to every post. A morning-of text post is a decision
to spend the golden hour in the corridor instead of the comments. Either is defensible;
