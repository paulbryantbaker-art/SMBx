# smbx Coach and smbx Crew — the build

**2026-08-22.** From a nine-agent pass: five design lanes, three sceptics attacking
the positioning from different angles, one completeness sweep. Two of the three
sceptics returned **weak** on the positioning I went in with. This is the version
that survived.

---

## 1. The positioning — what died

**Dead: "levels of ownership."** We run it / we staff it / you own it.

The family-office sceptic killed it in one line:

> *"Whose name is on the decision? Mine. It's always mine — that's what I'm paid
> for."*

Every principal answers identically, so the axis sorts nobody. Worse — ownership
of the mandate is **THE LINE, our compliance perimeter, dressed up as the buyer's
question.** That is inside-out: *"organising your product line around your own
regulatory geometry is a reason for me to wonder what you're worried about, not a
reason to buy."*

### What replaced it: exclusivity overflow

The strongest reframe came from the sceptic arguing hardest *against* adding the
lines at all. It starts from a fact that is already true and already published:

**We run one buy-side mandate per lane. That means we turn people away.**

Coach and Crew are not three doors on a homepage competing with the mandate. They
are **what the second caller in a taken lane gets offered instead of a no.**

> "We run one buy-side mandate per lane, through close, and we take a piece of the
> deal only when the deal is ours to run. If your lane is already taken we don't
> turn you away — we put the same department to work under your direction, either
> as the analyst bench your team is missing or as a standing quarterly review of
> your own pipeline. Neither one ever carries a success fee."

**Why this is the right shape for this practice specifically:**

- **It needs no bench.** You never advertise six seats you cannot fill. You offer
  what you can staff, to the person in front of you, when their lane is closed.
- **It cannot dilute.** Dev stays the only thing on the landing page and the only
  thing the Acquisition Engine sells. Coach and Crew never appear in the chrome.
- **It cannot cannibalise.** They are reached only through a door that opens when
  Dev is *unavailable* — so nobody chooses the cheap one over the mandate.
- **Scarcity does the selling.** "Your lane is taken" is the most persuasive
  sentence in the practice and it currently ends the conversation.

⚠️ **The cost, stated plainly:** this makes Coach and Crew feel like consolation
prizes. They will be harder to sell as products in their own right, and a
Coach client who knows they were second choice is a weaker reference. If you want
them as first-class offerings, you take the dilution risk back on.

### The axis, if you do put them on the page
All three sceptics converged: sell on **how much of the work you hand us** — never
on who owns it. That is a distinction buyers already purchase across in every
other vendor category, and it returns different answers from different people.

## 2. smbx Coach

### The bright line — the best single idea in the pass
Coach is bounded by **who touches the counterparty**, not by hours.

> "In Coach we work with your team. We never speak to a seller, an owner, a
> broker, a lender, or their counsel — that is a mandate."

Three observable tests: **no contact** (not even a listen-only seat on a
management call) · **no name out** (nothing leaving the client's building carries
smbX — the LOI, IC deck and lender packet are their documents, signed by their
people) · **no calendar** (we don't hold the close checklist or run the data room).

Cross the first two and it is a Dev mandate at the full schedule. Cross the third
and it is a Crew seat. **The boundary always comes with a price, never a refusal** —
the client always has a legitimate way to get what they want.

### What a quarter contains — 13 weeks, 14 live hours, 8 artifact runs
- **Week 1, 3 hrs — Method Install.** Buy-Box & Thesis Charter (2–4 pages,
  written: sector, size, geography, economics, deal-breakers, walk-away rules),
  the Corp Dev Operating Calendar, the Method Scorecard baseline.
- **Weeks 3/5/7/9/11/13, 90 min — six Deal Reviews**, same slot, biweekly. Each
  preceded 48 hours by ONE substrate artifact, read together in session.
- **Week 13, 2 hrs — Quarterly Pipeline Read.** Every live target scored against
  the charter, press/park/pass, scorecard delta.
- **Throughout — up to 8 artifact runs**, 2 business days each. All eight already
  exist as shipped skills: CIM teardown · add-back STAR · concentration forensics
  · dependency & transfer risk · deal scenarios · diligence plan · IC memo ·
  100-day plan.
- **Once — the Method Library**, theirs to keep.

**The discipline that stops it becoming a status meeting:** the client names the
deal 48 hours ahead or the slot converts to a pipeline session. **And the 8
artifact runs ARE the async lane — there is no second one.** An open Slack channel
is the single failure mode that eats the calendar.

### The buyer
A **$30–150M-revenue trades or industrial platform doing 2–6 tuck-ins a year that
has just put its first person in the corp-dev seat.** Three required facts: a live
mandate with budget, a person actually in the seat, a board expecting deals.

The commercial logic is precise: they just spent $150–250K fully loaded on that
hire and **cannot go back to the board three months later and buy a full mandate —
that reads as admitting the hire was wrong.** Coach is the thing they *can* buy.

**Anti-buyer: the first-time solo searcher.** No team, so "a team that needs
support" doesn't describe them. They consume unbounded calendar and fill the cap
with the lowest-value relationships in the funnel. Route them to Dev or the free
market read.

### 🔑 Price does not solve cannibalisation — eligibility does
No Coach retainer a coaching buyer would pay comes near a $5M Dev close ($210,000).
Any price is an arbitrage. **The defence is a checkable fact about the client:**

> Coach is sold only where the client already employs a **named, full-time person
> whose job is corporate development** — and we name that person in the letter.

That structurally excludes the entire Dev population, who by definition have
nobody in the seat. No person, no Coach; the honest answer is Dev or a Crew seat.

**Coach also never:** produces work product on a named live target · carries lane
exclusivity · survives a named-target trigger. And **the Dev retainer supersedes
Coach, never stacks** — one retainer, the higher one.

## 3. smbx Crew — sell ONE seat, not six

**Augmentability tracks inversely with hours spent in a room with someone who is
not our client.**

| Seat | Carried by the substrate | Sellable now? |
|---|---|---|
| CD Analyst | ~75–80% | **Yes** |
| Diligence Manager | ~65–70% | **Yes** |
| CD Project Manager | moderate | Later |
| Sourcing Associate | moderate | Later |
| Integration Manager | low | **No** |
| Interim Head of Corp Dev | low — it *is* the room | **No** |

### What makes it not "a contractor with ChatGPT"
Five mechanics, all checkable, all in the letter:
1. **The method is versioned, not personal** — the eight `smbx-*` skills are the
   seat's written SOP, so a replacement produces the same-shaped memo on day one.
2. **Arithmetic is engine-computed, never typed** — the `.deal.mts` spec is the
   artifact; hand-edits get overwritten on the next run, and the spec's git log
   *is* the negotiation history.
3. **Guard rails refuse rather than pass** — every figure traces to a source or a
   Derivations entry; `crossfoot.mts` recomputes printed working; a check that
   cannot see reports NOT ESTABLISHED instead of passing.
4. **Dual reporting line** — the client sets priority and sequence; smbX sets
   method, supervision and quality.
5. **Metered method on a cadence, never an FTE** — full seat 100 hrs/month,
   half 50. Monday plan call, Wednesday working session, **Friday register by
   noon Central without exception** — including weeks where nothing moved, which
   is the most valuable one, because it names what is overdue from the seller.

### The 60-day version
**One half-seat "Diligence Desk", held by Paul, sold to one acquirer with more
flow than they can process.** Not six seats. Not a bench.

## 4. What it costs — proposed, with the arithmetic shown

**smbx Coach — $9,000/quarter up front** (range $7,500–12,000). 60% of the Dev
retainer, ~$3,000/month, ~$450 per effective hour, and **10–14% of a fully loaded
in-house hire**. Capped at **four concurrent clients on one day a week** — the only
version one person can hold alongside mandates.

**smbx Crew — per seat, per month, minimum one quarter:**
Sourcing Associate $6,000 · CD Analyst $9,000 · CD Project Manager $9,000 ·
Diligence Manager $12,000 · Integration Manager $12,000 · Interim Head $25,000.
Generated by a rule: **55–70% of the fully loaded internal cost of the same seat.**

**Seats price on published THROUGHPUT expressed as a seat, never on hours** — an
hourly rate turns the AI substrate into a discount instead of a premium.

## 5. The two real risks, and neither is securities law

**1. "0 sell-side transactions. Ever." becomes falsifiable.** It is published on
the live site and the Track Record doctrine treats the stat set as law. Crew is the
first offering that puts that number in the hands of someone Paul does not
supervise, inside a business he does not control. **One incident makes a published
stat false.** Control: any instruction to perform sell-side, two-sided or
capital-raise work is a **material breach** — seat withdrawn immediately, quarter
not refunded — written in the termination section, not the recitals.

**2. Worker classification — and I got this wrong earlier.** I called it "ordinary
staffing-contract law, not securities law, and it's cheap." That was too casual.
**A corp-dev contractor supplied by a corp-dev firm fails prong B of the ABC test
almost by definition** — the work is not outside the usual course of our business.
In **California, Massachusetts, New Jersey and Illinois these seats are W-2 or they
are a wage claim.** That is a different lawyer from the securities one.

**3. One-buyer-per-target is a target-level rule; Coach and Crew produce conflicts
with no target in them.** Three shapes: target collision (undetectable — the Crew
client's target list never enters our systems) · lane collision (nothing fires, and
it degrades the paying mandate continuously) · protected party. **A Coach or Crew
client must be a hard block as a Dev target** — identity is certain there, not
inferred, which is exactly why the existing warn-never-block posture is wrong for
this case.

**The blind clearance desk:** a Crew seat submits every target name to Paul before
touching it and gets CLEARED / NOT CLEARED / HOLD **with no reason attached** — plus
a pre-agreed script for what they tell the client, written into the contract at
signature.

## 6. What would mis-sell a lead on Monday — three mechanical defects

1. **`practiceIntake.ts` `closeMessage()` promises lane exclusivity
   unconditionally** to every visitor. Coach carries none. A Coach client told
   that at intake has been mis-sold.
2. **`POST /api/practice/pricing` mails the Dev success-fee brochure to any lead**,
   including a Coach prospect — quoting contingent transaction compensation on an
   engagement where THE LINE forbids it, delivered by our own server.
3. **The `engagements` table has no `kind`**, so `successFee()` can run on a
   coaching row. Add `kind` and make it refuse.

**Two nobody raised that actually stop a sale:** procurement (W-9, COI naming GL
and E&O, MSA, cyber line, security questionnaire — a PE-backed acquirer signs none
of this without them), and **client-confidential documents defaulting into a git
repo**, where "we delete your files at termination" cannot be true, because history
is immutable without a rewrite and it is already replicated to GitHub.

## 7. Open decisions

1. **Overflow or front-door?** Reached only when a lane is taken (protects the
   mandate, makes them consolation prizes), or first-class on the site (dilution
   risk returns)?
2. **🔴 "Crew" is the wrong word for this market.** In HVAC, plumbing, elevator
   and roofing, *crew* means the field crew. To a trades acquirer "smbx Crew" reads
   as labour, not corp dev. Alternatives: smbx Bench · smbx Desk · smbx Seats.
3. **Is there a bench, or is Paul the seat?** Only the top two seats are sellable
   without one, and a contractor bench amends rule 2.
4. **Figures** — $9,000/quarter and the seat table are proposals, not decisions.
5. **Does counsel bless the Coach named-target trigger** — coaching adjacent to a
   live target, paid by a buyer, with no exclusivity?
